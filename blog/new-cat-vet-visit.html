Your New Cat's First Vet Visit: What to Expect

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · January 12, 2025

Congratulations on adopting your new feline companion! Whether you brought home a playful kitten or a mellow adult cat from Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, scheduling a veterinary checkup within the first week is one of the most important steps you can take. This first appointment establishes a health baseline, catches any concerns early, and builds a relationship between your cat and their new veterinarian.

Why the First Vet Visit Matters

When you adopt from Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, your cat arrives with up-to-date vaccinations, spay or neuter surgery, and a microchip. However, a post-adoption vet visit is still essential. The transition to a new home can be stressful, and stress sometimes unmasks underlying conditions that were not apparent in a shelter or foster environment. Your veterinarian will perform a comprehensive nose-to-tail examination, review your cat's medical records, and create a personalized wellness plan going forward.

What to Bring to the Appointment

Preparation makes the visit smoother for both you and your cat. Gather these items before heading out:

  • Adoption paperwork and medical records provided by Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, including vaccination history and spay/neuter confirmation
  • A secure carrier lined with a familiar blanket or towel that carries your scent
  • A stool sample collected within the past 24 hours, sealed in a plastic bag, for parasite screening
  • A list of questions about diet, behavior, litter preferences, or anything you have noticed since bringing your cat home
  • Notes on your cat's behavior at home, including appetite, litter box use, energy level, and any sneezing or discharge

What Happens During the Exam

A typical first visit lasts 30 to 45 minutes. Your veterinarian will check your cat's weight, temperature, heart and lung sounds, eyes, ears, teeth, skin, and coat. They will palpate the abdomen to check organ size and feel for any abnormalities. If your cat is due for any boosters or preventive treatments, those will be administered during this visit.

The vet may also recommend baseline blood work, especially for adult or senior cats. This provides a reference point for future comparison and can reveal early signs of kidney disease, diabetes, or thyroid imbalances that are not yet causing visible symptoms.

Reducing Stress on Vet Day

Many cats find car rides and veterinary offices stressful. Here are a few tips to make the experience easier:

  • Leave the carrier out at home for several days before the visit so your cat can explore it at their own pace
  • Spray the carrier with a feline pheromone product like Feliway about 15 minutes before departure
  • Cover the carrier with a light towel during transport to reduce visual stimulation
  • Speak calmly and avoid sudden movements in the exam room
  • Ask your vet about Fear Free practices if your cat is particularly anxious

Finding a Vet in the Portland Metro Area

If you are in the Milwaukie, Gladstone, or greater Portland area, there are many excellent veterinary clinics to choose from. Look for a practice that is comfortable with rescue cats and willing to work with the medical history provided by Cat's Meow Cat Rescue. The Oregon Veterinary Medical Association maintains a directory of licensed veterinarians throughout the state, which can help you find a clinic close to home.

Your new cat is counting on you to be their advocate. That first vet visit sets the tone for a lifetime of good health. If you have questions about your adopted cat's medical records or need guidance on choosing a veterinarian, do not hesitate to reach out to Cat's Meow Cat Rescue. We are always here to support our adopters and their furry family members.
