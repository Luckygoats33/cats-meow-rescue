Cat First Aid Basics Every Owner Should Know

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · June 29, 2026

Building a Cat First Aid Kit

Every cat owner should have a basic first aid kit that includes: gauze pads and rolls, adhesive tape (not the stick-to-fur kind), hydrogen peroxide (3%, for wound cleaning only, never to induce vomiting), styptic powder for bleeding nails, digital thermometer, blunt-tipped scissors, tweezers, and your veterinarian's emergency contact information. Store the kit somewhere accessible.

Handling Minor Wounds

For small cuts or abrasions, clean the wound gently with warm water or saline solution. Apply gentle pressure with gauze to stop bleeding. Do not use hydrogen peroxide on deep wounds or apply human antibiotic ointments without veterinary guidance. Monitor the wound for signs of infection (swelling, heat, discharge) and contact your vet if the wound does not improve within 24 hours.

When NOT to Treat at Home

Do not attempt to treat at home: deep puncture wounds (especially from cat bites, which are prone to serious infection), fractures or suspected broken bones, eye injuries, burns, poisoning, or any wound that is actively bleeding and will not stop with gentle pressure. These require immediate veterinary attention.

CPR for Cats

Cat CPR is a last-resort skill for when a cat has stopped breathing and has no heartbeat. Lay the cat on their right side, extend the head to open the airway, close the mouth and breathe gently into the nostrils, and compress the chest behind the front elbow at 100-120 compressions per minute. Alternate 2 breaths for every 30 compressions while someone drives you to the emergency vet.
