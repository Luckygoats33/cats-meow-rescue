Introducing a New Cat to a Multi-Cat Household

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · Milwaukie, Oregon

Adding a second (or third, or fourth) cat to your home can be wonderful — but introductions need to be handled carefully. A bad first impression can set the tone for months of conflict. Here's the protocol we recommend at Cat's Meow Cat Rescue.

Phase 1: Complete Separation (Days 1-3)

Keep your new cat in a separate room with the door closed. Your resident cat will smell the newcomer under the door, and that's exactly what you want. This scent exchange builds familiarity without the stress of face-to-face contact.

Phase 2: Scent Swapping (Days 3-7)

Swap bedding between the cats so each gets used to the other's scent. You can also rub a sock on one cat's cheek and leave it near the other's food bowl. Feed both cats on opposite sides of the closed door — they'll associate each other's scent with something positive (food!).

Phase 3: Visual Introduction (Days 7-14)

Use a baby gate or crack the door enough for the cats to see each other. Keep these sessions short (5-10 minutes) and reward calm behavior with treats. If either cat hisses, growls, or shows aggression, go back to Phase 2 for a few more days.

Phase 4: Supervised Meetings

When both cats seem relaxed during visual introductions, try short supervised meetings in a large room. Keep treats handy and have a plan to separate them quickly if needed. Gradual, positive experiences build lasting trust.

Tips for Success

Provide enough resources for everyone — multiple litter boxes (one per cat plus one), separate food stations, and multiple resting spots. Vertical space (cat trees, shelves) gives cats escape routes and territory without conflict. For more on understanding cat aggression during introductions, see our behavior guide.

The Ohio State University Indoor Cat Initiative has excellent science-backed resources on multi-cat households.
