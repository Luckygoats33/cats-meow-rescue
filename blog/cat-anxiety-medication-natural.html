Managing Cat Anxiety: Natural and Medical Options

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 6, 2026

Recognizing Anxiety in Cats

Anxious cats may hide excessively, over-groom to the point of creating bald patches, eliminate outside the litter box, become aggressive, lose their appetite, or vocalize excessively. Anxiety can be triggered by changes in the household, new pets, moving, construction noise, or past trauma. Many rescue cats at Cat's Meow Cat Rescue come to us with some degree of anxiety from their previous experiences.

Natural Remedies

Feliway (synthetic feline pheromone diffusers) can reduce stress-related behavior in many cats. Calming supplements containing L-theanine, tryptophan, or alpha-casozepine are available without a prescription. CBD oil formulated specifically for cats is gaining popularity, though research is still limited. Environmental enrichment like puzzle feeders, window perches, and interactive play can also reduce anxiety by providing mental stimulation.

When Medication Is Needed

For severe anxiety that does not respond to environmental changes and natural remedies, your veterinarian may prescribe medication such as fluoxetine (Prozac), gabapentin, or buspirone. These medications can significantly improve quality of life for severely anxious cats and are generally safe for long-term use. Your Portland-area veterinarian can help determine if medication is appropriate.

Creating a Calm Environment

Provide hiding spots (covered beds, boxes, high shelves), maintain a consistent routine, use soft music or white noise, keep the litter box clean and in a quiet location, and give your cat a room they can retreat to when overwhelmed. Time and patience are often the most effective treatments for anxiety in rescue cats.
