Toxic Foods for Cats: The Complete Oregon Kitchen Guide

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 12, 2026

Immediately Dangerous Foods

Some foods can cause rapid, life-threatening reactions in cats. These include onions and garlic (all forms, including powder), grapes and raisins, xylitol (an artificial sweetener found in sugar-free products), chocolate (especially dark and baking chocolate), alcohol, and raw yeast dough. If your cat ingests any of these, contact your veterinarian or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435 immediately.

Commonly Shared Foods That Are Harmful

Many foods that seem harmless are actually dangerous for cats. Milk and dairy products (most adult cats are lactose intolerant), raw eggs and raw meat (risk of Salmonella and E. coli), caffeine, macadamia nuts, and avocado can all cause problems ranging from digestive upset to organ damage. Resist the temptation to share your meals with your cat.

Oregon-Specific Seasonal Hazards

Oregon's agricultural bounty brings seasonal risks. Lily flowers, commonly found in Portland gardens and farmers markets, are extremely toxic to cats and can cause fatal kidney failure with even small exposures. Wild mushrooms that grow abundantly in Oregon's damp forests are dangerous if ingested. Holiday foods like turkey bones, rich gravies, and treats containing nutmeg also pose risks.

What Cats Can Safely Eat

If you want to treat your cat, small amounts of cooked chicken, turkey, or salmon (plain, no seasoning) are generally safe. Commercial cat treats are formulated to be both safe and nutritious. Always provide fresh water, and stick to high-quality commercial cat food as the primary diet.
