Diabetes in Cats: Signs, Treatment, and Oregon Resources

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · June 30, 2026

Understanding Feline Diabetes

Diabetes mellitus occurs when a cat's body cannot properly produce or respond to insulin, resulting in elevated blood sugar levels. It is most common in overweight, middle-aged to senior male cats. The condition is treatable and, in some cases, can even go into remission with proper management. Early detection is key.

Warning Signs

The classic signs of diabetes in cats are the "4 Ps": polydipsia (excessive thirst), polyuria (excessive urination), polyphagia (increased appetite), and unexplained weight loss despite eating more. You may also notice a change in gait, with the cat walking flat on their hocks (plantigrade stance), which indicates nerve damage from uncontrolled blood sugar.

Treatment and Management

Most diabetic cats require twice-daily insulin injections, which most owners can learn to administer easily. A high-protein, low-carbohydrate diet is essential. Regular blood glucose monitoring, either at home or at the vet, guides insulin dosage adjustments. Some cats achieve remission with early, aggressive treatment and dietary management, meaning insulin can eventually be discontinued.

Local Veterinary Support

Portland-area veterinarians are well-equipped to manage feline diabetes. Internal medicine specialists at VCA Northwest and DoveLewis can handle complex cases. Online communities like the Feline Diabetes Message Board provide peer support from experienced cat owners. Cat's Meow Cat Rescue has adopted out diabetic cats who are now thriving in loving homes.
