Cat Spraying vs. Inappropriate Urination: Know the Difference

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 4, 2026

How to Tell Them Apart

Spraying and inappropriate urination look different. When a cat sprays, they stand upright with their tail vertical and quivering, typically targeting vertical surfaces like walls, furniture, and door frames. The amount of urine is small. When a cat urinates inappropriately, they squat and deposit a normal-sized puddle on horizontal surfaces like beds, laundry, or floors. The causes and solutions for each are very different.

Understanding Spraying

Spraying is a territorial marking behavior, not a litter box problem. It is most common in intact males but can occur in any cat, including spayed females. Triggers include new cats in the household, stray cats visible through windows, changes in routine, and stress. Spaying and neutering eliminates spraying in approximately 90% of cases. For already-altered cats, reducing stress and blocking visual access to outdoor cats often resolves the issue.

Understanding Inappropriate Urination

When a cat urinates outside the litter box, the first step is always a veterinary exam to rule out urinary tract infections, crystals, or other medical conditions. If medical causes are excluded, the problem is usually environmental: a dirty litter box, wrong type of litter, box in a stressful location, insufficient number of boxes (rule: one per cat plus one), or inter-cat conflict in multi-cat households.

Cleaning Up

Use an enzymatic cleaner specifically designed for pet urine (Nature's Miracle, Rocco & Roxie, or similar). Standard household cleaners will not break down the urine proteins, and the lingering scent will attract the cat back to the same spot. Avoid ammonia-based cleaners, which smell like urine to cats.
