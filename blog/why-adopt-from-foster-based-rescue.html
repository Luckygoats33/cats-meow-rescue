Why Adopting from a Foster-Based Rescue Is Different

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 10, 2026

What Is a Foster-Based Rescue?

Unlike traditional brick-and-mortar shelters, foster-based rescues like Cat's Meow Cat Rescue house their animals in volunteer foster homes rather than in a facility. This means cats live in real home environments where they receive individualized attention, socialization with people and sometimes other pets, and a stress-free setting that allows their true personality to shine.

Better Behavioral Information

When a cat lives in a foster home, the foster parent can tell you exactly how the cat behaves in a domestic setting. Does the cat get along with other cats? How does it react to dogs? Is it good with children? Does it prefer to sleep on the bed or in a cat tree? This level of detail is nearly impossible to gather in a shelter environment where cats are stressed and not behaving normally.

Lower Stress, Healthier Cats

Shelter environments, even well-run ones, are inherently stressful for cats. The noise, unfamiliar smells, and close proximity to other animals can suppress immune systems and cause behavioral issues. Foster-based rescues eliminate these stressors. Our cats at Cat's Meow are healthier, happier, and better adjusted when they go to their forever homes because they have been living in a calm, loving environment.

How It Works at Cat's Meow

To adopt from Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, browse our available cats online, submit an application, and schedule a meet-and-greet at the cat's foster home. This gives you the chance to see the cat in a home environment and ask the foster parent any questions. We hold regular adoption events at PetSmart locations in the Clackamas and Portland area as well.
