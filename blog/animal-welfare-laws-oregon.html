Oregon Animal Welfare Laws Every Cat Owner Should Know

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · Milwaukie, Oregon

Oregon has some of the strongest animal welfare laws in the country. As a cat owner or rescue supporter, here are the key laws you should know.

Anti-Cruelty Laws

Oregon's animal cruelty laws (ORS 167.310-167.390) are among the toughest in the nation. Animal abuse is a felony, punishable by up to 5 years in prison and $125,000 in fines. Neglect — including failure to provide food, water, shelter, and veterinary care — is also criminal. Oregon was the first state to pass a law recognizing animals as victims of crime with individual rights.

Stray and Community Cats

Oregon law distinguishes between owned cats, stray cats, and community (feral) cats. Municipalities have varying ordinances regarding stray cats. In most jurisdictions, if you feed a cat regularly, you may be considered the owner and responsible for its care. TNR programs operate under specific guidelines — see our community cat guide.

Landlord-Tenant and Pets

Oregon law allows landlords to restrict pets, require pet deposits (up to one month's rent), and charge monthly pet rent. However, emotional support animals and service animals are protected under fair housing laws. Always get pet permission in writing before signing a lease.

Microchipping and Licensing

While Oregon doesn't have a statewide cat licensing requirement, some municipalities do. Microchipping isn't legally required but is strongly recommended — all Cat's Meow cats are microchipped before adoption.

Reporting Abuse

If you suspect animal cruelty or neglect, contact your local animal control agency or the Oregon Humane Society tip line. Reports can be made anonymously. In emergencies, call 911.

For the full text of Oregon's animal welfare statutes, visit the Oregon Legislature website.
