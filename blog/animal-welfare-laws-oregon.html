Oregon Animal Welfare Laws Every Cat Owner Should Know

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · April 20, 2025

Oregon's Strong Animal Protection Framework

Oregon has some of the strongest animal welfare laws in the United States. The Animal Legal Defense Fund has consistently ranked Oregon among the top states for animal protection legislation. As a cat owner or someone considering adoption, understanding these laws helps you be a responsible pet owner and a better advocate for animals in your community.

At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we believe that informed pet owners make better pet owners. Here is a guide to the key Oregon laws that affect cats and their people.

Anti-Cruelty Laws

Oregon's animal cruelty statutes are among the toughest in the country. Under Oregon Revised Statutes (ORS) Chapter 167, it is illegal to:

  • Neglect an animal by failing to provide adequate food, water, shelter, and veterinary care. This is classified as animal neglect in the first or second degree.
  • Intentionally harm or kill an animal without legal justification. Animal abuse in the first degree is a Class C felony in Oregon, carrying potential prison time.
  • Abandon an animal. Leaving a pet behind when you move or dumping a cat outdoors is a criminal offense.
  • Keep animals in unsanitary or dangerous conditions. Animal hoarding situations are taken seriously and can result in criminal charges.

Oregon is also one of the few states that recognizes animals as victims of crime rather than mere property, which strengthens enforcement and sentencing in cruelty cases.

Stray and Found Cat Laws

If you find a stray cat in Oregon, the law requires specific steps before you can claim ownership. Under ORS 498.405, a found domestic animal must be reported to local animal control or a law enforcement agency. The previous owner typically has a holding period to reclaim the animal.

In Clackamas County, which includes Milwaukie and Gladstone, Clackamas County Dog Services handles stray animal reports. If a cat is microchipped, they will attempt to contact the registered owner. If no owner is found within the holding period, the cat may be transferred to a rescue organization like Cat's Meow for rehoming.

Licensing and Identification Requirements

Unlike dogs, cats are not currently required to be licensed in most Oregon municipalities, including Milwaukie and Gladstone. However, there are important identification considerations:

  • Microchipping: While not legally mandated, microchipping is strongly recommended and is standard practice at responsible rescues. Every cat adopted from Cat's Meow is microchipped before going home.
  • Collar and ID tags: Even indoor cats benefit from wearing identification in case they escape.
  • Portland-specific rules: The City of Portland does require cat licensing within city limits. Check your local municipality's requirements if you are outside Milwaukie or Gladstone.

Landlord and Tenant Pet Laws

Oregon law does not prevent landlords from restricting pets in rental properties, but there are protections you should know about:

  • Pet deposits: Landlords may charge a refundable pet deposit in addition to a standard security deposit.
  • Service and emotional support animals: Under federal and state fair housing laws, landlords must make reasonable accommodations for tenants with disabilities who require assistance animals, including emotional support animals with proper documentation.
  • No-pet clauses: If your lease prohibits pets and you bring one in anyway, your landlord may have grounds for eviction. Always get written permission before bringing a cat into a rental.

Reporting Animal Cruelty or Neglect

If you witness animal cruelty or neglect in Oregon, you have a responsibility to report it. Here is how:

  1. Contact local law enforcement or animal control. In Clackamas County, call Clackamas County Dog Services. In Multnomah County, contact Multnomah County Animal Services.
  2. Document what you see with dates, photos, and detailed descriptions if it is safe to do so.
  3. Contact the Oregon Humane Society's investigations team at their tip line for cases within their jurisdiction.

At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we take animal welfare seriously. If you have questions about Oregon animal laws or need guidance on a situation involving a cat in distress, do not hesitate to reach out to us for help.
