Fostering a Pregnant Cat: Preparing for the Big Day

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 13, 2026

Setting Up the Nesting Area

A pregnant cat needs a quiet, private space where she can give birth and nurse her kittens without disturbance. Set up a large box or enclosed cat bed lined with clean towels or blankets in a warm, dimly lit room away from household traffic. Provide food, water, and a litter box within easy reach. Most cats prefer to give birth in a secluded spot.

Signs of Approaching Labor

In the 24-48 hours before labor, the cat may become restless, seek out her nesting area frequently, stop eating, vocalize more than usual, and begin nesting behavior (rearranging blankets). Her body temperature may drop slightly below 100 degrees Fahrenheit. You may also notice a mucus discharge. Labor typically begins within 24 hours of these signs.

During and After Birth

Most cats handle labor and delivery without any human intervention. Kittens arrive every 15-60 minutes, and the mother will clean each kitten, chew through the umbilical cord, and eat the placenta. Your job is to observe from a distance and intervene only if there is a problem, such as more than two hours between kittens, excessive bleeding, or a kitten the mother is not attending to.

Post-Birth Care

After delivery, the mother will nurse her kittens and keep them warm. Provide her with high-quality kitten food (which is higher in calories and nutrients) and plenty of water to support milk production. Contact Cat's Meow Cat Rescue immediately after the birth so we can schedule a vet check for the mother and kittens. The mother should be spayed after the kittens are weaned at 6-8 weeks.
