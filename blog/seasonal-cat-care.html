Seasonal Cat Care: Keeping Cats Safe Year-Round in Oregon

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · April 10, 2025

Oregon's climate brings distinct seasonal challenges for cat owners. From the wet, chilly winters of the Willamette Valley to the occasionally scorching summer days, each season requires a slightly different approach to keeping your cat healthy and comfortable. Here is our season-by-season guide from Cat's Meow Cat Rescue.

Spring: Allergies, Parasites, and Open Windows

As Oregon's famous rains begin to taper and flowers bloom, spring brings a few concerns for cat owners:

  • Flea and tick season begins. Start or renew your cat's flea prevention in early spring. Consult your veterinarian for a product that is safe for cats. Never use dog flea products on cats, as permethrin-based treatments are toxic and potentially fatal to felines.
  • Seasonal allergies can affect cats just like humans. Watch for excessive scratching, sneezing, or watery eyes, and consult your vet if symptoms persist.
  • Open windows feel wonderful in spring, but make sure all screens are secure before letting the fresh air in. Cats can push through loose screens and fall or escape.

Summer: Heat, Dehydration, and Fireworks

While Oregon summers are generally mild compared to other states, heat waves do occur. During hot spells:

  • Ensure your cat always has access to fresh, cool water. Consider a pet water fountain to encourage drinking.
  • Provide cool resting spots away from direct sunlight. Tile floors, cooling mats, and shaded window perches help cats regulate their temperature.
  • Watch for signs of heatstroke: panting, drooling, lethargy, and vomiting. Flat-faced breeds like Persians and older cats are at higher risk.
  • Fourth of July fireworks terrify many cats. Close windows and curtains, play calming music, and provide hiding spots. Never take your cat to a fireworks display.

Fall: Rodent Poisons and Shorter Days

As temperatures drop, rodents seek shelter indoors, and many homeowners set out traps and poisons. Rodenticides are extremely dangerous to cats, both through direct ingestion and through eating a poisoned mouse or rat (secondary poisoning). Use cat-safe pest control methods and keep all bait stations completely inaccessible to your cat.

Fall also brings shorter days. Cats are sensitive to changes in daylight and may adjust their sleep patterns. This is normal, but if you notice significant changes in appetite, energy, or behavior, a vet visit is warranted to rule out other causes.

Winter: Cold Weather, Antifreeze, and Holiday Hazards

Oregon winters are wet and cold, with occasional ice storms in the Portland metro area. Winter cat care priorities include:

  • Antifreeze (ethylene glycol) is sweet-tasting and deadly. Even a teaspoon can kill a cat. Clean up spills immediately and store containers securely. Consider switching to propylene glycol-based antifreeze, which is less toxic.
  • Holiday decorations pose risks: tinsel can cause intestinal blockages, poinsettias and lilies are toxic, and ornaments can shatter and cut paws. Keep your tree secured and skip the tinsel if you have cats.
  • Space heaters and fireplaces should have guards or barriers. Cats love warmth and may get too close, risking burns or singed whiskers.
  • If you have outdoor community cats in your neighborhood, check wheel wells and under car hoods before starting your engine on cold mornings. Cats often seek warmth near engines.

Year-Round Essentials

Regardless of season, maintain these basics: annual veterinary checkups, up-to-date vaccinations, consistent flea prevention, and fresh water always available.
