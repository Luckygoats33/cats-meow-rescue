Enrichment Ideas for Foster Cats on a Budget

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 3, 2026

Free Enrichment

The best enrichment often costs nothing. Cardboard boxes make excellent hiding spots and play structures. Paper bags (with handles removed) provide crinkly entertainment. Crumpled paper balls, empty toilet paper rolls, and ping pong balls are all engaging toys. A sock filled with dried catnip provides hours of rolling and kicking fun.

DIY Puzzle Feeders

Cut holes in a cardboard box or plastic container and fill with kibble. The cat must fish out the food, mimicking hunting behavior. Muffin tins with kibble in each cup and tennis balls on top create another challenge. Ice cube trays with treats frozen in water provide cooling enrichment during Portland summers.

Environmental Enrichment

Rotate the cat's environment by moving furniture, adding new boxes, or changing the location of perches. Open a window (with secure screen) for fresh air and outdoor sounds. Play bird videos on a tablet or phone. These small changes prevent environmental boredom without spending a penny.

Social Enrichment

Your time and attention are the most valuable enrichment you can offer. Ten minutes of interactive play with a wand toy, a grooming session, or simply sitting nearby while the cat explores does more for socialization than any toy could. Cat's Meow Cat Rescue provides basic toys and supplies for fosters, but your presence is the enrichment that matters most.
