Returning an Adopted Cat: When and How to Do It Right

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 5, 2026

It Happens More Than You Think

Returning an adopted cat is more common than most people realize, and it is not something to feel ashamed about. Life circumstances change, unexpected allergies appear, or sometimes the cat and household are simply not a good fit. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we would always rather have a cat returned to us than abandoned or surrendered to an overcrowded shelter.

When to Consider Returning

Valid reasons for returning include severe allergies that do not respond to management, aggression toward children or other pets that cannot be resolved with behavioral intervention, and major life changes like job loss, homelessness, or health crises. We ask that you give the adoption at least two to four weeks before deciding, as most adjustment issues resolve during that time.

How Cat's Meow Handles Returns

Contact us as soon as you realize the adoption is not working. We will take the cat back into our foster network, no questions asked and no judgment. The cat will be placed in a new foster home and made available for adoption again. We are a no-kill rescue, and every cat in our care is safe regardless of how many times they need to be rehomed.

Trying Before Returning

Before returning, contact us for support. Many issues that feel insurmountable, like litter box problems, hiding behavior, or inter-cat tension, have straightforward solutions. Our team has decades of combined experience and can often help resolve problems with a few adjustments.
