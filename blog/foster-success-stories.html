Foster Success Stories from Cat's Meow Cat Rescue

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 2, 2025

Behind every adoption at Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, there is a foster parent who made it possible. Our foster families in the Milwaukie, Gladstone, and greater Portland area open their homes and hearts to cats who need a second chance, and the results speak for themselves. Here are some of the stories that remind us why this work matters.

Maple: From Feral to Family Cat

Maple was trapped as part of a TNR effort in Clackamas County, but at just five months old, our team believed she had a chance at socialization. Her foster parent, a retired teacher, committed to spending hours each day simply sitting in the room with her, reading aloud from novels while Maple hid behind the toilet tank.

After three weeks, Maple began emerging to eat while her foster mom was present. By week five, she accepted treats from an outstretched hand. By week eight, Maple was sleeping at the foot of the bed and purring when scratched behind the ears. She was adopted by a quiet couple in Lake Oswego who report that Maple now greets them at the door every evening. Her foster parent went on to socialize four more feral kittens.

Oliver and Bean: The Bonded Brothers

Oliver and Bean arrived at Cat's Meow as eight-week-old kittens with severe upper respiratory infections. Both were congested, sneezing, and had crusty eyes that required medicated drops four times daily. Their foster family — a mom, dad, and two elementary-school-aged kids — divided the medication duties into a team effort.

The kids learned to prepare the warm compresses for the kittens' eyes while mom and dad handled the actual drops. Within three weeks, both kittens were healthy, bouncing off the walls, and completely inseparable. Cat's Meow listed them as a bonded pair, and they were adopted together by a family in Milwaukie who had recently lost their elderly cat and were ready to fill their home with kitten energy again.

Duchess: The Senior Survivor

At fourteen years old, Duchess was surrendered when her owner entered assisted living. She arrived terrified, refusing to eat, and hiding under a blanket in her carrier for the first 48 hours. Senior cats are among the hardest to place in foster care and the hardest to adopt out, but Duchess's foster parent was determined.

With patience, a heated bed, and a steady diet of rotisserie chicken to stimulate her appetite, Duchess began to come around. By her second week in foster care, she was following her foster dad from room to room and demanding lap time every evening. Duchess was adopted by a retiree in Gladstone who specifically wanted a calm, affectionate senior companion. Her adopter sends monthly photo updates, and Duchess now rules the household from her favorite armchair.

Tiny Tim: The Bottle Baby Miracle

Tiny Tim was found alone in a parking lot at less than a day old, still with his umbilical cord attached. Weighing barely three ounces, his chances were slim. His foster parent, an experienced bottle baby volunteer, committed to round-the-clock feedings every two hours and kept him warm with a heating pad and a stuffed animal heartbeat simulator.

Tim fought through the critical first week, gained weight steadily, and hit every milestone. By eight weeks old, he weighed two and a half pounds and had the personality of a cat ten times his size — fearless, playful, and obsessed with attacking shoelaces. He was adopted by a young couple who had followed his journey through Cat's Meow's social media posts and applied to adopt him before he was even old enough to be listed.

Why We Share These Stories

Every one of these cats came to Cat's Meow Cat Rescue in a situation that could have ended badly. Maple could have lived her life as a feral cat struggling to survive Oregon winters. Oliver and Bean might not have recovered from their infections without dedicated medical foster care. Duchess could have languished in a shelter environment that would have broken her spirit. Tiny Tim almost certainly would not have survived his first night without a foster parent willing to set alarms every two hours.

These stories are not exceptional — they are what happens every day when ordinary people choose to foster. If you see yourself in any of these foster parents, we would love to hear from you. Cat's Meow Cat Rescue is always looking for new foster families, and the next success story could be yours.
