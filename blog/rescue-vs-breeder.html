Rescue Cat vs. Breeder Cat: Making the Right Choice

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · March 28, 2025

Understanding Your Options

When you decide to bring a cat into your life, you face a fundamental choice: adopt from a rescue or purchase from a breeder. Both paths lead to a new feline companion, but the implications of that choice extend far beyond your own household. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we believe in giving people honest, factual information so they can make a decision they feel good about.

This article is not about guilt or judgment. It is about understanding what each option truly involves so you can choose wisely.

What You Get When You Adopt from a Rescue

Rescue cats come with a unique set of advantages that many prospective owners do not realize:

  • Comprehensive veterinary care included: At Cat's Meow, every cat is spayed or neutered, fully vaccinated, dewormed, treated for fleas, and microchipped before adoption. This medical package alone would cost $300 to $500 at a private veterinarian.
  • Known personality: Because our cats live in foster homes rather than cages, we can tell you exactly how a cat behaves in a home environment. Does the cat get along with dogs? Is the cat a lap cat or an independent explorer? Our foster parents know.
  • Wide selection: Rescues have cats of every age, breed mix, coat color, and personality type. Kittens, adults, seniors, playful cats, mellow cats, we have them all.
  • Post-adoption support: Reputable rescues stay available to answer questions and help with the transition after you bring your cat home.
  • Saving a life: Every adoption directly saves the cat you take home and frees a foster spot for the next cat waiting to be rescued.

What You Get When You Buy from a Breeder

Purchasing from a breeder offers certain things that some people prioritize:

  • Predictable breed traits: If you want a specific purebred with known breed characteristics, a reputable breeder can provide that. However, mixed-breed cats are often healthier and more genetically diverse.
  • Kitten availability: Breeders typically sell kittens at specific ages. That said, rescues also frequently have kittens available, especially during spring and summer kitten season.
  • Higher upfront cost: Purebred cats from breeders typically cost $500 to $2,000 or more, compared to adoption fees that usually range from $75 to $200.

It is also important to distinguish between responsible breeders who prioritize health and welfare and backyard breeders or kitten mills that cut corners and contribute to overpopulation.

The Bigger Picture

Beyond personal preference, the choice between rescue and breeder has broader consequences. The United States has more cats than it can house. Shelters are consistently at or over capacity, and rescue organizations like Cat's Meow operate on shoestring budgets to fill the gaps.

When you adopt, you become part of the solution. You reduce demand for commercially bred animals, you support the volunteer-driven rescue network, and you give an animal a second chance it would not otherwise have.

Making Your Decision

If you are weighing your options, we encourage you to visit a local rescue before making a decision. Come to one of Cat's Meow Cat Rescue's PetSmart adoption events in the Milwaukie or Gladstone area and meet our cats in person. Many adopters tell us they came in unsure and left with a cat that was nothing like what they imagined, but exactly what they needed.

You can also browse our available cats online to see who is currently looking for a home. Whatever you decide, choosing responsibly is what matters most.
