Adopting a Cat When You Have Kids: Tips for a Happy Home

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · Milwaukie, Oregon

Cats and kids can be the best of friends, but a successful introduction requires preparation on both sides. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we help families find cats who are kid-tested and parent-approved.

Choose the Right Cat

Look for cats described as confident, social, and tolerant. Cats who've been socialized with children in their foster home are ideal. At Cat's Meow, our foster parents note which cats do well with kids of different ages. Avoid very shy or skittish cats for households with young children — the unpredictable noise and movement can stress them.

Teach Kids Cat-Appropriate Behavior

Before the cat comes home, teach children how to interact gently: soft pets (no grabbing or pulling), quiet voices near the cat, and recognizing when a cat wants to be left alone. Explain that a cat's tail twitching or ears flattening means "I need space." Even toddlers can learn to be gentle with coaching.

Supervise Early Interactions

Never leave young children alone with a new cat. Supervise all interactions for at least the first few weeks. Create "cat-only" zones where the cat can retreat if they feel overwhelmed — a room with a baby gate that has a cat-sized opening works perfectly.

Age-Appropriate Responsibilities

Involving kids in cat care teaches responsibility and deepens their bond. Toddlers can help fill the water bowl (with help). Kids 5-8 can measure food portions. Older children can scoop litter and play with interactive toys. Everyone benefits from the routine.

Kittens vs. Adult Cats for Families

Many families default to wanting a kitten, but an adult cat is often a better choice with young children. Kittens are fragile and scratch when overstimulated. A confident, sturdy adult cat can handle the energy of a household with kids. For more on choosing, see our guide to choosing the right cat.
