Chronic Kidney Disease in Cats: Early Detection Saves Lives

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 3, 2026

Understanding CKD in Cats

Chronic kidney disease is one of the most common conditions in aging cats, affecting approximately one in three cats over the age of 15. The kidneys gradually lose their ability to filter waste products from the blood, leading to a buildup of toxins. While CKD is not curable, early detection and management can significantly extend your cat's life and quality of life.

Early Warning Signs

The earliest signs of CKD are often subtle: increased water consumption, increased urination, and gradual weight loss. As the disease progresses, you may notice decreased appetite, vomiting, bad breath with a chemical odor, lethargy, poor coat condition, and constipation. Annual blood work for cats over 7 years old can detect CKD before clinical signs appear.

Treatment and Management

Treatment focuses on slowing progression and managing symptoms. A prescription kidney diet (lower in phosphorus and protein) is the cornerstone of management. Subcutaneous fluid therapy, phosphate binders, anti-nausea medications, and blood pressure management may also be needed. Many cats with CKD live years after diagnosis with proper care.

Monitoring at Home

Track your cat's water consumption, weight, and litter box output. Sudden changes in any of these should prompt a veterinary visit. Smart litter boxes and pet water fountains with consumption tracking can help you monitor changes objectively. Your Portland veterinarian can establish a monitoring schedule appropriate for your cat's stage of disease.
