How to Handle Saying Goodbye to Your Foster Cat

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · Milwaukie, Oregon

Saying goodbye to a foster cat is the hardest part of fostering — and also the most important. Every goodbye means a cat found their forever home, and space opened for another life to be saved.

It's Okay to Feel Sad

Grief when a foster cat leaves is normal and healthy. It means you did your job — you formed a bond that prepared this cat for their forever home. Allow yourself to feel the sadness without guilt. Most experienced foster parents say the goodbye never gets easy, but it does get more manageable.

Strategies That Help

Take photos and videos throughout the foster period — you'll treasure these later. Request updates from the adopter (many are happy to share). Focus on the impact: your foster work directly saved a life. Consider taking a short break between fosters if you need to recharge emotionally.

Remember Why You Foster

For every cat you say goodbye to, another cat gets a chance. Cat's Meow can only rescue as many cats as we have foster homes for. Your ability to let go — with grace and love — is literally what makes rescue possible. You're not losing a cat; you're giving one a future.

The "Foster Fail" Option

If you truly can't let go of a particular cat, you always have the option to foster-to-adopt. But use this option thoughtfully — if you adopt every foster, you can't keep fostering, and your long-term impact on cat rescue diminishes. Most foster parents find a balance that works for them.

What Our Foster Parents Say

"The adoption day tears are the price of admission for the joy of fostering. And they're worth it every time." — Cat's Meow foster parent since 2019.

For emotional support, our foster coordinator is always available to talk. Best Friends Animal Society has a foster community where you can connect with others who understand.
