Choosing the Best Cat Food: A No-Nonsense Guide

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 5, 2026

What Cats Actually Need

Cats are obligate carnivores, meaning they require nutrients found only in animal tissue. The most important thing to look for in cat food is a named animal protein (chicken, turkey, salmon) as the first ingredient. Avoid foods where the first ingredients are corn, wheat, soy, or unnamed "meat by-products." Cats also need taurine, an amino acid essential for heart and eye health that must come from animal sources.

Wet vs. Dry Food

Wet food is generally considered more biologically appropriate because of its high moisture content (70-80% water), which supports urinary and kidney health. Dry food is more convenient and cost-effective. Many veterinarians recommend a combination of both. At minimum, provide at least some wet food in your cat's diet, especially as they age.

Reading Labels

The ingredient list is more important than marketing claims. "Natural," "premium," and "gourmet" have no regulatory definitions for pet food. Look for an AAFCO (Association of American Feed Control Officials) statement confirming the food is "complete and balanced." Check that protein comes from identifiable animal sources, not generic "meat" or "animal digest."

Portland Options

Portland pet supply stores like Mud Bay employ nutrition-educated staff who can help you choose appropriate food. Many stores allow returns if your cat rejects a food, reducing the risk of expensive mistakes.
