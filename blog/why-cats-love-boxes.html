Why Cats Love Boxes: The Science Explained

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 5, 2026

Safety and Security

In the wild, small enclosed spaces protect cats from predators approaching from behind or the sides. A box provides that same sense of security in a domestic environment. Research from the University of Utrecht found that shelter cats given hiding boxes showed significantly lower stress levels and adapted to their environment faster than cats without boxes.

Temperature Regulation

Cardboard is an excellent insulator. A cat's thermoneutral zone (the temperature range where they do not need to expend energy to stay warm or cool) is 86-97 degrees Fahrenheit, much higher than typical room temperature. A cardboard box helps trap body heat, creating a cozy microclimate that matches the cat's preferred temperature.

Predatory Advantage

Boxes also serve as ambush points. A cat inside a box can observe their surroundings through the opening while remaining hidden, replicating the hunting strategy of waiting for prey to pass by. This is why cats in boxes will often pounce at passing feet or toys.

Using Boxes to Help Your Cat

At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we always provide boxes for newly arrived foster cats. A simple cardboard box in a quiet corner can help a stressed cat feel safe during the adjustment period. For adopted cats, keeping a box or two in your home provides a permanent stress-reduction tool that costs nothing.
