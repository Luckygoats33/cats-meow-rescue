TNR Programs in Portland: How Trap-Neuter-Return Helps Community Cats

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 15, 2026

What Is TNR?

Trap-Neuter-Return (TNR) is the humane method of managing feral and community cat populations. Cats are humanely trapped, spayed or neutered by a veterinarian, ear-tipped for identification, and returned to their outdoor colony. TNR is endorsed by the ASPCA, Humane Society, and the majority of animal welfare organizations as the most effective and ethical approach to community cat management.

TNR in Portland

Portland has an active TNR community supported by organizations like the Feral Cat Coalition of Oregon, which provides free spay/neuter services for community cats. Multnomah County and Clackamas County both support TNR as their official community cat management strategy. Cat's Meow Cat Rescue participates in TNR efforts and socializes kittens from feral colonies for adoption when possible.

How TNR Works

Community cat caretakers identify colonies, set humane traps baited with food, transport the trapped cats to a participating veterinary clinic for surgery, allow a brief recovery period, and return the cats to their colony. The ear tip (a small, painless removal of the tip of one ear) prevents the cat from being trapped again. Over time, the colony stabilizes and gradually decreases in size through natural attrition.

How You Can Help

If you notice a group of feral cats in your Milwaukie, Gladstone, or Portland neighborhood, contact the Feral Cat Coalition of Oregon or Cat's Meow Cat Rescue. We can help you organize a TNR effort, provide traps, and coordinate veterinary care. Never simply relocate feral cats, which is stressful and often ineffective, as new cats quickly fill the vacancy.
