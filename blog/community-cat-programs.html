Community Cat Programs: TNR in Milwaukie and Beyond

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · Milwaukie, Oregon

Community cats (also called feral or free-roaming cats) are a significant part of Oregon's cat population. Trap-Neuter-Return (TNR) is the most humane and effective way to manage these populations.

What Is TNR?

TNR involves humanely trapping community cats, having them spayed/neutered and vaccinated, ear-tipping (removing a small tip of one ear for identification), and returning them to their outdoor colony. This prevents reproduction, reduces nuisance behaviors (fighting, spraying), and stabilizes the population over time.

Why TNR Works

Traditional trap-and-remove approaches create a "vacuum effect" — new cats move into the territory. TNR avoids this by keeping existing cats in place while preventing new kittens. Studies show TNR reduces colony sizes by 16-32% within 2 years. It's endorsed by the ASPCA, Humane Society, and virtually every major animal welfare organization.

TNR in Our Area

The Feral Cat Coalition of Oregon provides low-cost spay/neuter services and TNR training. They've sterilized over 90,000 cats since 1995. Multnomah, Clackamas, and Washington counties all support TNR as the preferred management method for community cats.

How You Can Help

If you have community cats in your neighborhood: contact the Feral Cat Coalition for guidance and supplies. They'll loan traps and teach you the process. Cat's Meow also assists with TNR efforts in the Milwaukie/Gladstone area — if you're feeding community cats and need help getting them fixed, reach out to us.

Socializable Kittens

During TNR operations, we often find kittens young enough to be socialized for adoption. These kittens go into our foster program for socialization and eventual placement in forever homes. It's a perfect intersection of community cat management and kitten rescue.
