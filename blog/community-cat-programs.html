Community Cat Programs: TNR in Milwaukie and Beyond

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · May 10, 2025

What Are Community Cats?

Community cats, sometimes called feral or free-roaming cats, are unowned cats that live outdoors in neighborhoods, parks, and commercial areas. They may be truly feral, meaning they have had little to no human socialization, or they may be friendly strays that were once pets. In either case, community cats are a natural part of the urban landscape in the Portland metro area and throughout Oregon.

Understanding the difference between feral and stray cats matters because it determines the best approach for their care. Stray cats can often be socialized and adopted. Truly feral cats, however, are typically not candidates for indoor living and are best served by Trap-Neuter-Return programs.

How Trap-Neuter-Return Works

Trap-Neuter-Return, commonly known as TNR, is the most humane and effective method for managing community cat populations. The process follows a straightforward cycle:

  1. Trap: Cats are humanely trapped using baited live traps, typically box traps designed specifically for cats.
  2. Neuter: Trapped cats are brought to a veterinary clinic for spaying or neutering. While under anesthesia, they also receive a rabies vaccination and an ear tip, a small clipping of the left ear that signals the cat has been fixed.
  3. Return: After a short recovery period, cats are returned to their original outdoor location where a caretaker continues to provide food and monitor the colony.

Over time, TNR stabilizes and gradually reduces colony sizes because no new kittens are born. Nuisance behaviors like spraying, fighting, and yowling also decrease significantly after cats are altered.

TNR Resources in the Milwaukie and Portland Area

Several organizations in the Portland metro area support TNR efforts for community cats:

  • Feral Cat Coalition of Oregon (FCCO): A leading resource for TNR in the state, offering low-cost spay and neuter clinics specifically for community cats.
  • Cat Adoption Team (CAT): In addition to shelter adoptions, CAT runs an active TNR program and provides community cat resources.
  • Clackamas County Animal Services: Offers guidance and sometimes trap loans for residents dealing with community cat colonies in areas like Milwaukie and Gladstone.
  • Oregon Humane Society: Provides low-cost spay and neuter services and educational materials about community cat management.

At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we work alongside these organizations. When we encounter friendly strays during TNR efforts, we bring them into our foster program for socialization and eventual adoption.

What You Can Do to Help Community Cats

If you notice community cats in your Milwaukie, Gladstone, or Portland neighborhood, there are several constructive steps you can take:

  • Contact a local TNR organization rather than calling animal control. TNR is more effective and humane than removal.
  • Provide food and water to community cats if you choose to be a caretaker, keeping feeding stations clean and consistent.
  • Build or place outdoor shelters to protect cats from Oregon's cold, rainy winters. Simple insulated shelters can be made from storage bins and straw.
  • Never relocate community cats. Moving cats to unfamiliar territory is dangerous for them and ineffective, as new cats will fill the vacated area.

The Evidence Behind TNR

Decades of research and community experience have demonstrated that TNR is the only method proven to humanely reduce community cat populations over time. Traditional trap-and-remove approaches create a vacuum effect, where new cats move into the territory left behind. TNR avoids this by keeping the existing, now-sterilized colony in place, naturally preventing new cats from establishing themselves.

If you would like to learn more about community cat programs in Oregon or get involved with TNR in your area, Cat's Meow Cat Rescue can help connect you with the right resources. Reach out to us anytime.
