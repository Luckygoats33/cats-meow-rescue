Why Your Cat Bites When You Pet Them

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 3, 2026

What Is Petting Aggression?

Your cat is purring contentedly while you pet them, and then suddenly they turn and bite. This behavior, known as petting-induced overstimulation or petting aggression, is one of the most common complaints from cat owners. The cat is not being mean or unpredictable. They are telling you that their sensory threshold has been exceeded and they need the stimulation to stop.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Before biting, most cats give several warnings that owners miss. Watch for skin twitching or rippling along the back, tail flicking or thumping, ear rotation to the sides or back, whiskers moving forward into an alert position, and a stiffening of the body. When you see any of these signs, stop petting immediately and let the cat move away.

Prevention Strategies

Learn your cat's tolerance threshold. Some cats enjoy five minutes of petting while others reach their limit in thirty seconds. Pet in preferred areas (usually the cheeks, chin, and base of the ears) and avoid the belly and base of the tail, which are sensitive zones for many cats. Short, frequent petting sessions are better than long ones. Let the cat initiate and end the contact.
