Cat Behavior

Why Your Cat Brings You Dead Animals

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 8, 2026

The Hunting Instinct

All domestic cats retain their hunting instinct regardless of how well-fed they are. Hunting behavior is triggered by movement and is independent of hunger. Even the most pampered indoor cat will stalk, pounce, and catch insects, toys, and occasionally real prey if given the opportunity. This instinct is hardwired and cannot be trained away.

Why They Bring It to You

There are two leading theories. The first is that your cat sees you as a poor hunter who needs help and is providing food, similar to how mother cats bring prey to their kittens. The second is that your cat views you as part of their social group and is sharing resources, a behavior observed in feral cat colonies where adults share prey.

How to Respond

Do not punish your cat for bringing you prey. They are acting on deep instincts and genuinely believe they are doing something helpful or social. Calmly dispose of the prey, clean the area, and wash your hands. If your cat is an indoor-outdoor cat, this is yet another reason to transition them to indoor-only living, which also protects wildlife.

Redirecting the Instinct

Provide ample opportunities for your cat to hunt appropriate targets. Interactive wand toys, motorized mouse toys, puzzle feeders that make them work for food, and play sessions that mimic the stalk-chase-catch-eat cycle can satisfy the hunting instinct without involving real prey. Indoor cats at Cat's Meow Cat Rescue thrive with regular play enrichment.
