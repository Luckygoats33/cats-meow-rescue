Cat Vaccination Schedule: A Complete Guide

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · Milwaukie, Oregon

Vaccinations are one of the most important tools for keeping your cat healthy. Here's what every cat owner in Oregon should know about feline vaccines.

Core Vaccines (Required)

FVRCP protects against feline viral rhinotracheitis, calicivirus, and panleukopenia — all highly contagious and potentially fatal diseases. Kittens receive a series of shots starting at 6-8 weeks, with boosters every 3-4 weeks until 16 weeks of age. Adults need boosters every 1-3 years depending on your vet's protocol.

Rabies is required by Oregon law. The first vaccine is given at 12 weeks or older, with a booster one year later, then every 1-3 years depending on the vaccine type.

Non-Core Vaccines (Situational)

FeLV (Feline Leukemia Virus) vaccination is recommended for outdoor cats, cats in multi-cat households, or cats with unknown exposure histories. Our FIV/FeLV guide explains these diseases in detail.

What Cat's Meow Provides

All cats adopted from our rescue come up-to-date on FVRCP and rabies vaccinations. Kittens will need to complete their booster series on schedule. We'll provide a complete vaccination record at adoption so your vet knows exactly where your cat stands.

After Vaccination

It's normal for cats to be slightly lethargic for 24-48 hours after vaccination. A small lump at the injection site can occur and usually resolves within 2-3 weeks. Contact your vet if your cat has difficulty breathing, facial swelling, or prolonged vomiting after a vaccine — these are rare but serious reactions.

For the most current vaccination guidelines, refer to the AAHA/AAFP Feline Vaccination Guidelines.
