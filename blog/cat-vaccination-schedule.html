Cat Vaccination Schedule: A Complete Guide

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · February 8, 2025

Vaccinations are one of the simplest and most effective ways to protect your cat from serious, potentially life-threatening diseases. Whether you have just adopted a kitten or welcomed an adult cat into your home, understanding which vaccines are needed and when they should be given will help you stay on top of your cat's preventive care.

Core Vaccines: Essential for Every Cat

Core vaccines are recommended for all cats regardless of lifestyle, because the diseases they prevent are widespread, highly contagious, or particularly dangerous. The core vaccines for cats include:

  • FVRCP (Feline Viral Rhinotracheitis, Calicivirus, Panleukopenia) — This combination vaccine protects against three serious diseases. Panleukopenia, sometimes called feline distemper, is often fatal in unvaccinated kittens. Rhinotracheitis and calicivirus cause upper respiratory infections that can lead to chronic health issues.
  • Rabies — Required by Oregon state law for all cats. Rabies is fatal once symptoms appear and poses a public health risk. Your cat must receive a rabies vaccine to be in compliance with local regulations.

Non-Core Vaccines: Based on Lifestyle and Risk

Non-core vaccines are given based on your cat's individual risk factors, including whether they go outdoors, interact with other cats, or live in a multi-cat household.

  • FeLV (Feline Leukemia Virus) — Recommended for all kittens and for adult cats that go outdoors or live with FeLV-positive cats. FeLV suppresses the immune system and can lead to cancer and other serious conditions.
  • Bordetella — Sometimes recommended for cats in shelters, boarding facilities, or multi-cat environments where respiratory infections spread easily.
  • Chlamydia — Occasionally recommended for cats at high risk of exposure, particularly in multi-cat settings.

Kitten Vaccination Timeline

Kittens need a series of vaccinations starting at 6 to 8 weeks of age, with boosters every 3 to 4 weeks until they are about 16 weeks old. A typical schedule looks like this:

  1. 6-8 weeks: First FVRCP, first FeLV
  2. 10-12 weeks: Second FVRCP, second FeLV
  3. 14-16 weeks: Third FVRCP, first rabies vaccine
  4. 1 year: FVRCP booster, rabies booster

After the initial series, FVRCP is typically boosted every 3 years for indoor cats, while rabies boosters follow a schedule determined by the vaccine type and Oregon state requirements.

Keeping Up with Boosters

After the initial vaccine series, staying current on boosters is straightforward. Most veterinary clinics in the Portland metro area will send reminder cards or emails when your cat is due. Keeping a simple calendar reminder is another easy way to make sure nothing slips through the cracks. If your cat ever needs to board, visit a groomer, or stay at a pet-sitting facility, proof of current vaccinations will typically be required.

If you have questions about your adopted cat's vaccination history, Cat's Meow Cat Rescue is happy to help you track down records and coordinate with your veterinarian.
