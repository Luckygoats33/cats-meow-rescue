Why Cats Hide and When to Be Concerned

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · April 28, 2025

You bring your new rescue cat home, open the carrier, and they immediately disappear under the bed. Hours pass, then a full day, and you begin to worry. Is something wrong? At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we reassure every adopter that hiding is one of the most normal and expected behaviors when a cat enters a new environment. But it is also worth understanding when hiding crosses the line from normal coping to a sign that something needs attention.

Normal Reasons Cats Hide

Hiding is a deeply ingrained survival instinct. In the wild, a cat who cannot hide is a cat who is exposed to predators. Domestic cats retain this instinct, and hiding is their primary coping mechanism for stress and uncertainty. Common triggers include:

  • New environments: Moving to a new home is one of the most stressful events in a cat's life. Expect hiding for the first three to seven days, sometimes longer for timid cats.
  • Visitors and noise: Unfamiliar people, loud conversations, parties, and construction sounds can send even confident cats into hiding.
  • New pets or household members: A new baby, roommate, or another animal disrupts the established territory and routine.
  • Thunderstorms and fireworks: Oregon winters bring plenty of storms, and summer brings fireworks around the Fourth of July. Both are common hiding triggers.
  • Veterinary visits: The stress of a carrier ride and examination can cause hiding for hours or even days afterward.

How to Help a Hiding Cat

The most important thing you can do for a hiding cat is resist the urge to pull them out or force interaction. Forcing a frightened cat into the open destroys trust and extends the hiding period. Instead:

  1. Set up a small, quiet room with food, water, litter box, and a cozy hiding spot (a cardboard box with a blanket works well).
  2. Spend time in the room reading, working on your phone, or just sitting quietly. Let the cat get used to your presence without any pressure.
  3. Talk softly so the cat learns your voice. Leave a worn t-shirt near their hiding spot so they become familiar with your scent.
  4. Place treats near the hiding spot, gradually moving them closer to you over days.
  5. Let the cat set the pace. Some cats emerge within 48 hours; others take two to three weeks. Both timelines are normal.

When Hiding Signals a Problem

While hiding itself is normal, certain patterns warrant attention:

  • Sudden onset in a previously social cat: If a cat who normally greets you at the door suddenly starts hiding, this can indicate pain, illness, or a frightening experience. A veterinary exam is recommended.
  • Not eating or drinking while hiding: A cat who hides and refuses food for more than 24 hours needs medical attention. Cats can develop hepatic lipidosis (fatty liver disease) after just a few days without eating.
  • Hiding combined with other symptoms: Lethargy, vomiting, diarrhea, labored breathing, or changes in litter box habits alongside hiding suggest a medical issue.
  • Chronic hiding beyond the adjustment period: If a cat is still hiding after a month in a calm, stable home, consult with your veterinarian about anxiety treatments or behavioral support.

Creating Healthy Hiding Options

Rather than trying to eliminate hiding, provide intentional hiding spots throughout your home. Cat tunnels, covered beds, shelves with sides, and simple cardboard boxes give your cat a sense of security and control. A cat with access to safe hiding places is actually a more confident cat — they explore more freely knowing they have a retreat available.
