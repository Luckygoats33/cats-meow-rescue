Why Do Cats Knead? The Science Behind Making Biscuits

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 12, 2026

The Kitten Connection

Kneading, often called making biscuits, originates in kittenhood. Kittens knead their mother's belly while nursing to stimulate milk flow. This behavior is deeply associated with comfort, safety, and the warmth of the mother-kitten bond. When adult cats knead, they are essentially reverting to this comforting behavior, indicating that they feel safe and content.

Why Adult Cats Knead

Adult cats knead for several reasons. They may be expressing contentment and relaxation, marking territory with scent glands in their paw pads, preparing a sleeping surface by softening it, or self-soothing when stressed. Some cats knead more than others, and the intensity can range from gentle pressing to vigorous paw pushing with extended claws.

Managing Kneading Behavior

Kneading is healthy and should not be discouraged. If your cat's claws make kneading uncomfortable, keep nails trimmed or place a thick blanket on your lap. Never punish a cat for kneading, as it is an expression of trust and comfort. If your cat kneads furniture, redirect them to a designated blanket or cat bed.
