🧠 Cat Behavior

Signs of Anxiety in Cats and How to Help

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · Milwaukie, Oregon

Cats experience anxiety more commonly than most owners realize. Because cats hide their distress, recognizing the signs early makes a huge difference in their quality of life.

Signs of Cat Anxiety

Excessive grooming (especially leading to bald patches), hiding more than usual, litter box avoidance, decreased appetite, increased aggression, excessive vocalization, and compulsive behaviors (repeated pacing, tail-chasing). Any significant behavior change could indicate anxiety.

Common Triggers

Environmental changes (moving, renovations, new furniture), new household members (humans or animals), changes in routine, loss of a companion (human or animal), insufficient resources in multi-cat homes, and past trauma (common in rescue cats).

Creating a Calming Environment

Provide predictability: consistent feeding times, regular play sessions, and stable routines. Offer safe spaces where your cat can retreat without being disturbed. Cat trees, covered beds, and high shelves give anxious cats a sense of security. Feliway diffusers release synthetic feline pheromones that many cats find calming.

Enrichment as Anxiety Treatment

Boredom and under-stimulation contribute to anxiety. Interactive play, puzzle feeders, window perches, and environmental variety help redirect anxious energy into positive activity. Music designed for cats (yes, it exists) can also have a calming effect.

Professional Help

If anxiety is severe, your vet may recommend anti-anxiety medication alongside behavioral modification. Medications like gabapentin or fluoxetine can be game-changers for chronically anxious cats. Never give your cat human anxiety medication without veterinary guidance.

For rescue cats with anxiety rooted in past experiences, our bonding guide for shy cats offers patience-focused techniques. The American Association of Feline Practitioners has detailed stress-reduction resources.
