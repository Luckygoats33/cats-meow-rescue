The Complete Guide to Cat Scratching Posts

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 4, 2026

Why Cats Scratch

Scratching is not destructive behavior. It is an essential feline need that maintains claw health by removing the outer sheath, stretches muscles and tendons, marks territory through scent glands in the paws, and provides stress relief. You cannot and should not try to stop a cat from scratching. Instead, provide appropriate surfaces and redirect scratching to those surfaces.

Choosing the Right Post

The post must be tall enough for your cat to fully stretch (at least 32 inches for adult cats), sturdy enough not to wobble, and covered in a material cats prefer. Sisal rope and sisal fabric are the most popular materials. Some cats prefer corrugated cardboard or bare wood. Avoid carpet-covered posts, which can confuse cats about which carpeted surfaces are acceptable to scratch.

Placement

Place scratching posts near the furniture your cat currently scratches, near sleeping areas (cats often stretch and scratch after waking), and near entry points to rooms your cat frequents. Cats use scratching to mark territory, so posts in prominent locations are more appealing than posts hidden in corners. Multiple posts in a multi-room home are ideal.

Training Your Cat

Attract your cat to the post by rubbing catnip on it, dangling a toy near it, and rewarding any interaction with the post using treats and praise. If your cat scratches furniture, gently redirect them to the post. Cover furniture targets temporarily with double-sided tape or aluminum foil. Never punish scratching. Declawing is not recommended and is being increasingly banned, including in some Oregon jurisdictions.
