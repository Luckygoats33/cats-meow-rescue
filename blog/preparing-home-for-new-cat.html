Preparing Your Home for a New Cat: A Complete Checklist

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · February 8, 2025

Congratulations on your decision to adopt! Whether you are bringing home a kitten or a senior cat from Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, a little preparation goes a long way toward making the transition smooth and stress-free. Here is everything you need to do before your new cat walks through the door.

Set Up a Safe Room

Every newly adopted cat needs a dedicated space to decompress. A spare bedroom, bathroom, or even a large walk-in closet works perfectly. This room should contain:

  • A litter box (uncovered is usually best for a new cat)
  • Food and water bowls placed away from the litter box
  • A cozy bed or blanket in a quiet corner
  • A hiding spot such as a cardboard box turned on its side or a covered cat bed
  • A scratching post or pad

This room will be your cat's home base for the first few days to a week. It gives them a chance to learn the smells and sounds of your home without feeling overwhelmed by a large, unfamiliar space.

Cat-Proof Your Home

Before letting your new cat explore beyond the safe room, walk through your home and address potential hazards:

  • Toxic plants: Lilies, poinsettias, and pothos are among the most common household plants that are dangerous to cats. The Oregon Humane Society and ASPCA both maintain lists of toxic plants you can reference.
  • Small objects: Hair ties, rubber bands, string, and small toys can cause intestinal blockages if swallowed.
  • Cords and wires: Secure electrical cords and blind strings. Cats, especially kittens, love to chew on dangling cords.
  • Open windows: Make sure all windows have secure screens. Oregon homes often have older screens that may pop out easily.
  • Cleaning products and medications: Store these in cabinets with secure latches.

Essential Supplies to Have Ready

Stock up on these items before adoption day so you are not scrambling at the last minute:

  1. Quality cat food: Ask Cat's Meow what your cat has been eating in foster care and start with the same brand to avoid digestive upset.
  2. Litter and litter box: One box per cat plus one extra is the standard recommendation.
  3. Scratching surfaces: Both vertical posts and horizontal pads give your cat options.
  4. Interactive toys: Wand toys, crinkle balls, and puzzle feeders keep cats mentally stimulated.
  5. A carrier: You will need this for the ride home and future vet visits. Leave it out with the door open so your cat can get comfortable with it over time.
  6. Enzymatic cleaner: For any accidents during the adjustment period.

Schedule a Vet Visit

Even though Cat's Meow Cat Rescue ensures every cat is spayed or neutered, vaccinated, and microchipped before adoption, you should schedule a wellness visit with your own veterinarian within the first two weeks. This establishes a baseline and builds a relationship with a vet who will know your cat over time. The Milwaukie and Gladstone area has several excellent feline-friendly veterinary clinics.

Prepare Your Family

If you have children, talk to them about how to interact gently with the new cat. Explain that the cat may hide at first and that this is completely normal. Set ground rules: no chasing, no picking up without permission, and always let the cat come to them. If you have other pets, plan for a gradual introduction rather than a face-to-face meeting on day one.

A well-prepared home means a calmer, happier cat and a smoother start to your life together. If you have questions about preparing for your specific adopted cat, our volunteers and foster families at Cat's Meow are always available to help.
