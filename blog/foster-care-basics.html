Foster Care 101: Everything You Need to Know

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · January 12, 2025

Every year, thousands of cats in the Portland metro area need temporary homes while they wait for adoption. Foster care is the backbone of rescue work, and at Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, our foster families in the Milwaukie and Gladstone communities make it possible for us to save more lives than our facility alone could ever handle. If you have ever thought about fostering a cat, this guide covers everything you need to know to get started.

What Is Cat Fostering?

Fostering means providing a temporary home for a cat or kitten until they are ready for adoption. Foster parents offer a safe, calm environment where cats can decompress from shelter stress, recover from illness or surgery, or simply grow old enough to be spayed or neutered. You are not committing to keeping the cat forever — you are giving them a crucial stepping stone on the path to their permanent family.

Foster stays can range from as short as two weeks to several months, depending on the cat's needs. Kittens too young for adoption may stay until they reach two pounds and can be spayed or neutered, while a shy adult cat might need a few months of socialization before they are ready to meet adopters at our PetSmart partner locations.

What Does Cat's Meow Provide?

We believe fostering should be accessible to everyone, which is why Cat's Meow Cat Rescue covers the essential costs associated with your foster cat's care:

  • Veterinary care: All medical expenses including vaccinations, spay/neuter surgery, and treatment for any illness or injury
  • Food and litter: We provide cat food and litter for your foster, though many foster parents choose to supplement with their own preferred brands
  • Supplies: Basic supplies like carriers, bowls, and blankets can be borrowed from our inventory
  • Ongoing support: Our experienced foster coordinators are available by phone, text, or email to answer questions and troubleshoot any issues

What Do You Need to Provide?

The most important things you provide are your time, your patience, and a safe space in your home. Ideally, you should have a spare room or bathroom where the foster cat can be set up initially, especially if you have resident pets. This quiet space gives new fosters time to adjust without feeling overwhelmed.

You will also need to be able to transport your foster cat to veterinary appointments and, eventually, to adoption events or our PetSmart locations in the area. A willingness to communicate regularly with your foster coordinator about the cat's progress is essential for matching each cat with the right adopter.

The Foster Process at Cat's Meow

Getting started is straightforward:

  1. Fill out a foster application on our website or at one of our adoption events
  2. Complete a brief orientation where we go over expectations, care basics, and emergency protocols
  3. Get matched with a cat based on your experience level, home setup, and preferences
  4. Welcome your foster home and begin the rewarding work of helping them thrive
  5. Stay in touch with your coordinator as the cat progresses toward adoption readiness

Why Fostering Matters

The impact of fostering cannot be overstated. Every cat that goes into a foster home opens up space for another cat to be pulled from a dangerous situation, whether that is an overcrowded municipal shelter, a hoarding case, or life on the streets of Clackamas County. Foster cats also tend to get adopted faster because foster parents can provide detailed personality profiles that help match cats with the right families.

Many of our long-time volunteers say fostering is one of the most fulfilling things they have ever done. Yes, it can be bittersweet to say goodbye when your foster finds their forever home, but knowing you played a direct role in saving that life is a feeling that never gets old. If you are ready to open your heart and your home, Cat's Meow Cat Rescue is ready to support you every step of the way.
