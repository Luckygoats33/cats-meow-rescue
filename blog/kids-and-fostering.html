Fostering Cats with Kids in the House

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · June 14, 2025

Fostering a cat when you have children is not only possible — it can be one of the most enriching experiences for your entire family. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, many of our best foster families include kids of all ages. Children who grow up fostering develop empathy, responsibility, and a deep understanding of animal welfare that stays with them for life. Here is how to make it work safely and successfully.

Age-Appropriate Involvement

Every child can participate in fostering, but their role should match their developmental stage:

  • Toddlers (1-3 years): Too young for direct care responsibilities, but can be taught gentle touching with constant supervision. Keep foster cats in a room with a door that toddlers cannot open on their own.
  • Preschoolers (4-5 years): Can help fill water bowls, select toys for the cat, and practice sitting quietly near a shy foster. Always supervised.
  • School-age (6-10 years): Ready for real responsibilities like scooping litter (with hand-washing after), measuring food portions, and helping with socialization through gentle play.
  • Tweens and teens (11+): Can handle most foster care tasks independently, including medication administration with training. Many teens find fostering deeply meaningful and it looks great on college applications and volunteer resumes.

Teaching Kids to Respect Cat Body Language

The most important skill your children need is the ability to read a cat's signals. Teaching kids when a cat wants to be left alone prevents scratches and builds mutual trust. Go over these basics with your children before your foster cat arrives:

  • Happy signs: Slow blinking, purring, kneading, relaxed body, tail held high
  • Warning signs: Flattened ears, twitching tail, dilated pupils, hissing, growling, or an arched back
  • The golden rule: If the cat walks away, let them go. Never chase, corner, or pick up a cat who is trying to leave.

Role-playing these scenarios with stuffed animals before the real cat arrives can help younger children internalize the lessons.

Setting Ground Rules

Clear family rules make fostering smoother for everyone. Cat's Meow recommends establishing these guidelines before your foster cat comes home:

  1. The foster cat's room is a quiet zone — no running, yelling, or rough play in or near that space
  2. Only adults handle the cat for the first few days until the cat is settled and comfortable
  3. Hands must be washed after handling the cat, cleaning the litter box, or touching cat supplies
  4. The foster cat is not a toy — gentle hands only, and always on the cat's terms
  5. If the cat hides, we respect their space and give them time

Posting these rules on the foster room door serves as a visual reminder for kids and visiting friends alike.

Choosing the Right Foster Cat for Your Family

Not every foster cat is a good match for a home with children, and that is perfectly okay. When you apply to foster with Cat's Meow, let your coordinator know the ages of your children so we can match you with a cat who is comfortable around kids. Generally, the best matches are:

  • Friendly, confident adult cats who are not easily startled
  • Kittens over eight weeks old who are socialized and playful
  • Cats with a known history of living with children

We typically recommend that families with young children avoid fostering feral cats, bottle baby neonates, or cats with significant medical needs until they have some fostering experience.

The Lessons Kids Learn

Beyond the practical experience of animal care, fostering teaches children lessons that textbooks cannot. They learn that love sometimes means letting go when the foster cat goes to their forever home. They see firsthand how patience and kindness can transform a frightened animal into a trusting companion. They understand that their family's small act of opening their home makes a real difference in the world.

Many of our foster families in the Milwaukie and Gladstone communities tell us that fostering has become their favorite family activity. If your kids have been asking for a cat and you are not ready for a permanent commitment, fostering is a wonderful way to share the experience of caring for an animal while making a meaningful contribution to Cat's Meow Cat Rescue.
