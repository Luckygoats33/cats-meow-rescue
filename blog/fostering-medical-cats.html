Fostering Cats with Medical Needs: What to Expect

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · March 22, 2025

Some of the cats who come through Cat's Meow Cat Rescue need more than just a warm bed and regular meals. They need medical foster care — a temporary home where they can recover from surgery, receive treatment for illness, or manage a chronic condition until they are healthy enough for adoption. Medical fostering is not as intimidating as it sounds, and Cat's Meow covers all veterinary costs and provides hands-on training for every medical foster placement.

Types of Medical Foster Cases

Medical fosters encompass a wide range of situations. Understanding the most common types can help you decide which cases fit your comfort level:

  • Post-surgical recovery: Cats recovering from spay/neuter, dental extractions, or orthopedic procedures who need rest, medication, and monitoring for one to three weeks
  • Upper respiratory infections: Very common in rescue cats, especially kittens. These cats need a warm, quiet space, twice-daily medication, and sometimes nebulizer treatments
  • Ringworm treatment: A fungal skin infection that requires daily medication and lime sulfur dips over four to six weeks. Highly treatable but requires diligence and good hygiene
  • Eye conditions: Kittens with eye infections or injuries often need medicated drops multiple times daily until they heal
  • Chronic conditions: Some cats require ongoing management of conditions like diabetes, hyperthyroidism, or FIV. These cats may need longer foster stays until an adopter experienced with their condition is found

What Cat's Meow Provides for Medical Fosters

We never ask foster parents to bear the financial burden of medical care. For every medical foster placement, Cat's Meow provides:

  • All medications with written instructions and dosing schedules
  • Veterinary appointments at our partner clinics in the Milwaukie and Portland area
  • Specialized supplies such as e-collars, syringes, nebulizers, or wound care materials
  • Direct phone or text access to your foster coordinator for questions between vet visits
  • Training on medication administration before the cat comes home with you

You will never be expected to handle a medical situation you have not been prepared for. Our coordinators will walk you through every step before you take a medical foster home.

Tips for Administering Medication

Giving a cat medication is a skill that improves quickly with practice. Here are some tips from experienced Cat's Meow medical fosters:

  • Pill pockets: Soft treat pouches that hide pills inside. Many cats eat them without noticing the medication.
  • Burrito wrap: Gently wrapping the cat in a towel with only their head exposed gives you control while keeping them calm.
  • Liquid medications: Use a syringe inserted into the side of the mouth, behind the canine teeth. Dispense slowly to prevent choking.
  • Eye drops: Approach from behind so the cat does not see the dropper coming. One drop, quick reward, done.
  • Consistency: Give medications at the same times every day to build a routine. Cats are creatures of habit, and a predictable schedule reduces stress.

The Emotional Side of Medical Fostering

Medical fostering can be emotionally challenging. You may care for a cat whose outcome is uncertain, or you may watch a kitten struggle through a difficult illness before finally turning the corner. The lows can be lower, but the highs are extraordinary. Seeing a cat who arrived sick and scared become healthy, confident, and ready for adoption is a feeling that stays with you.

It is also important to recognize that not every medical foster case has a happy ending. Cat's Meow supports foster parents through loss with compassion and understanding, and we will never place a cat with a terminal prognosis without being upfront about the situation.

Why Medical Fosters Are So Needed

Cats with medical needs are the hardest to place in foster care, yet they are often the ones who need it most. A shelter environment adds stress that slows healing, while a quiet foster home dramatically improves recovery outcomes. If you are willing to learn, have a spare room, and can commit to a medication schedule, you have what it takes to be a medical foster parent. Cat's Meow Cat Rescue will handle the rest.
