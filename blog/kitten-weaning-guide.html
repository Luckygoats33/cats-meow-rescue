Kitten Weaning Guide: From Bottle to Bowl

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 4, 2026

When to Start Weaning

Kittens are typically ready to begin weaning around 3-4 weeks of age, when their teeth start coming in. Signs of readiness include biting the bottle nipple, showing interest in the foster parent's food, and the ability to walk steadily. Weaning is a gradual process that usually takes 2-3 weeks to complete.

The Gruel Stage

Begin by mixing kitten milk replacer with a small amount of wet kitten food to create a gruel consistency. Place the mixture in a shallow dish and let kittens explore it. Expect mess. Kittens will walk through it, sit in it, and get it everywhere before they figure out how to eat from a dish. Continue offering bottles as needed.

Transitioning to Solid Food

Gradually reduce the liquid in the gruel over 7-10 days until kittens are eating wet food with minimal added liquid. Introduce dry kitten food alongside wet food around 5-6 weeks. Ensure fresh water is always available in a shallow dish. By 6-8 weeks, most kittens should be fully weaned and eating independently.

Common Weaning Challenges

Some kittens resist weaning. Try different flavors and textures of food. Gently dab a small amount on their lips to encourage tasting. If a kitten refuses food and loses weight, continue supplementing with bottles and contact Cat's Meow Cat Rescue for guidance. Patience is key during this developmental milestone.
