Seasonal Allergies in Oregon Cats: What to Watch For

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 14, 2026

Common Allergens in Oregon

Oregon's wet climate and abundant vegetation create a year-round allergy environment. Common triggers include grass pollen (especially from ryegrass, which Oregon produces more of than any other state), tree pollen from alder, birch, and cedar, mold spores thriving in damp conditions, and dust mites that proliferate in humid indoor environments. Cats, like humans, can develop sensitivities to any of these.

Signs of Allergies in Cats

Unlike humans who sneeze, cats with environmental allergies typically show skin symptoms. Watch for excessive scratching or grooming, hair loss (especially around the head, neck, and belly), red or irritated skin, recurring ear infections, and watery eyes. If your cat shows these symptoms seasonally, allergies are a likely culprit.

Treatment Options

Your veterinarian can diagnose allergies through skin testing or blood panels. Treatment options include antihistamines, corticosteroids, immunotherapy (allergy shots), medicated shampoos, and omega-3 fatty acid supplements. Keeping your cat indoors during peak pollen season (May-July in Oregon) and using HEPA air filters can also reduce exposure.

When to See the Vet

If your cat is scratching to the point of creating open sores, losing significant amounts of fur, or showing signs of infection (swelling, discharge, fever), see your veterinarian promptly. Portland has several veterinary dermatology specialists including DoveLewis and Veterinary Dermatology of Portland for complex cases.
