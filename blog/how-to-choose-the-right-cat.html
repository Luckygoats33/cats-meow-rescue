How to Choose the Right Cat for Your Family

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · January 12, 2025

Bringing a cat into your family is one of the most rewarding decisions you can make. But with so many cats waiting for homes at shelters and rescues like Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, how do you find the one that fits your life? The answer starts not with breed or color, but with understanding your household and matching it to a cat's personality.

Start with Your Lifestyle

Before you browse adoptable cats, take an honest look at your daily routine. A cat's personality matters far more than appearance when it comes to long-term happiness for both of you.

  • Work schedule: If you work long hours, an independent adult cat may be a better match than a high-energy kitten that needs constant stimulation.
  • Activity level: Active families often do well with playful, curious cats, while quieter households might prefer a calm lap cat.
  • Other pets: If you already have a dog or another cat, you need an adoptee with a history of getting along with other animals.
  • Children: Families with young kids should look for cats described as patient, tolerant, and social. Our adoption counselors at Cat's Meow can guide you toward family-friendly cats.

Personality Matching: The Key to a Great Fit

At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, every cat in our care is observed and evaluated by experienced foster families and volunteers. We get to know each cat's temperament, which means we can tell you whether a cat is a bold explorer, a shy sweetheart who needs patience, or a social butterfly who greets everyone at the door.

When you visit our cats at our PetSmart partner location or at adoption events in the Milwaukie and Gladstone area, spend time sitting quietly with a cat. Let them come to you. A cat that approaches, rubs against you, or settles in your lap is showing genuine comfort and interest. That connection matters more than any photo on a website.

Age Considerations

Many adopters default to kittens, but cats of every age have something wonderful to offer:

  • Kittens (under 1 year): Energetic, playful, and endlessly entertaining, but they require significant time, training, and kitten-proofing. They also change as they mature, so their adult personality may differ from the tiny ball of energy you bring home.
  • Adult cats (1-7 years): What you see is what you get. Their personality is established, they are typically past destructive phases, and they settle into new homes more quickly.
  • Senior cats (8+ years): Calm, affectionate, and often overlooked. Senior cats tend to bond deeply with their adopters and make outstanding companions for quieter households.

Questions to Ask the Rescue

A good rescue will welcome your questions. Here are some to bring when you meet a potential match at Cat's Meow:

  1. How does this cat behave around other animals and children?
  2. What is their energy level like throughout the day?
  3. Do they have any medical conditions or special needs I should know about?
  4. How did they behave in their foster home?
  5. Are they bonded with another cat who should be adopted alongside them?

Our volunteers and foster families are always happy to share what they know, because a successful match means the cat stays in their forever home.

Trust the Process

Choosing a cat is not like shopping for a product. Sometimes the cat you did not expect ends up being the perfect companion. Be open to meeting cats outside your initial preferences. The scruffy tabby in the corner might turn out to be the most devoted cuddle buddy you have ever known.

At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we are committed to helping you find the right fit. Every adoption includes spay/neuter, vaccinations, and microchipping, so you can focus on what matters most: building a loving relationship with your new family member.
