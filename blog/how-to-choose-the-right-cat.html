How to Choose the Right Cat for Your Family

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · Milwaukie, Oregon

Choosing a cat is one of the most exciting decisions you'll make — but it's also one that deserves careful thought. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we've matched hundreds of cats with families across the Portland metro area, and we've learned that the best adoptions start with honest self-reflection.

Consider Your Lifestyle First

Before you fall in love with a face on Petfinder, think about your daily routine. Do you work from home, or are you gone 10 hours a day? A high-energy kitten needs more interaction than a laid-back senior cat. If you're a first-time cat owner, an adult cat with an established personality can be an easier transition than a kitten who's still figuring out the world.

Personality Over Appearance

It's tempting to choose a cat based on looks alone — that fluffy orange tabby is irresistible — but personality compatibility matters far more for long-term happiness. Our foster parents spend weeks learning each cat's quirks, preferences, and energy level. We categorize our cats as sweet (gentle and affectionate), sassy (confident and playful), or chaotic (high-energy adventurers) to help you find your match.

Age Matters

Kittens are adorable but demanding. They need supervision, multiple feedings, and patience during their "destroy everything" phase. Adult cats (1-7 years) are often the sweet spot — still playful but with established habits. And don't overlook senior cats (7+). They're typically calmer, already litter-trained, and profoundly grateful for a warm home. At Cat's Meow, our senior adoption fee is just $75.

Single Cat or Multi-Cat Household?

Some cats thrive as the only pet, while others need a feline companion. If you already have cats at home, consider adopting one that's been successfully living with other cats in their foster home. Our guide to multi-cat introductions can help you navigate the process.

The Meet-and-Greet

Once your application is approved, we'll arrange a meet-and-greet at the foster home or one of our PetSmart adoption events. This is your chance to interact with the cat in a low-pressure environment. Don't worry if the cat is shy at first — most rescue cats need time to warm up in new surroundings.

Ready to start your search? Browse our adoptable cats or fill out an adoption application. The ASPCA's adoption resources are also a great starting point for first-time adopters.
