Honoring Cats Who Have Crossed the Rainbow Bridge

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · June 30, 2026

Grieving Is Normal

The loss of a cat is a genuine loss. Cats are family members who share our daily lives for 15-20 years. The grief that follows their death is real, valid, and sometimes overwhelming. Do not let anyone tell you it is "just a cat." Your grief reflects the depth of your bond, and that bond was real and meaningful.

Ways to Honor Their Memory

Consider creating a memory box with photos, their collar, a paw print, and a favorite toy. Plant a memorial garden or tree. Make a donation to Cat's Meow Cat Rescue in their name. Write about your cat in a journal or share memories on social media. Commission a portrait from one of Portland's many talented pet portrait artists.

When You Are Ready Again

There is no timeline for when you should consider adopting again. Some people are ready within weeks while others need years. When the time feels right, adopting another cat is not a replacement for the one you lost. It is a new relationship, a new bond, and a way to honor the love you shared by giving that love to another cat in need.

Support Resources

The Association for Pet Loss and Bereavement offers a free phone hotline and online chat support. The Oregon Humane Society has pet loss support resources. Cat's Meow Cat Rescue understands the pain of loss and is here for our adopters during this difficult time.
