The First Week with Your Adopted Cat: What to Expect

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · March 5, 2025

You have done the paperwork, gathered your supplies, and brought your new cat home from Cat's Meow Cat Rescue. Now what? The first week is a critical adjustment period for both you and your cat. Understanding what to expect day by day will help you stay patient and set the stage for a lifelong bond.

Days 1-2: The Quiet Period

Your cat will likely hide. This is completely normal and not a sign that something is wrong. After the stress of being transported to a new environment with unfamiliar smells and sounds, most cats retreat to a safe spot and stay there.

During these first two days:

  • Keep your cat in their designated safe room with the door closed.
  • Visit the room a few times a day, but do not force interaction. Sit on the floor, read a book aloud, or simply be present.
  • Make sure food, water, and the litter box are easily accessible, even if your cat is hiding under the bed.
  • Speak softly. Avoid loud music, vacuum cleaning near the room, or sudden noises.

Some cats, especially those who thrived in foster homes, may surprise you by coming out to explore within hours. Others may take several days. Both responses are normal.

Days 3-4: Emerging and Exploring

By the third or fourth day, most cats begin venturing out of their hiding spot when the room is quiet. You may notice them exploring at night or in the early morning when the house is still. Signs of growing comfort include:

  • Eating and drinking regularly
  • Using the litter box consistently
  • Coming out to investigate when you enter the room
  • Slow blinking at you, which is a cat's way of saying "I trust you"

Resist the urge to rush the process. Let your cat set the pace. Offering treats by hand can help build positive associations with your presence.

Days 5-7: Building the Routine

By the end of the first week, many cats are ready to explore beyond the safe room. Open the door and let them wander at their own pace. Keep the safe room available as a retreat they can return to whenever they feel overwhelmed.

This is also when you should begin establishing daily routines:

  • Feeding schedule: Cats thrive on predictability. Feed at the same times each day.
  • Play sessions: Two 10-15 minute interactive play sessions per day help burn energy and build your bond.
  • Quiet time: Respect your cat's need for solitude. Not every moment needs to be interactive.

Common First-Week Concerns

New adopters often worry about behaviors that are actually normal adjustment responses:

  • Not eating for 24-48 hours: Stress can suppress appetite. If your cat has not eaten anything after 48 hours, contact your vet or reach out to Cat's Meow for advice.
  • Hiding constantly: Some shy cats need two weeks or more before they feel safe. Patience is essential.
  • Litter box avoidance: Make sure the box is clean, accessible, and in a quiet location. If problems persist beyond a few days, consult your veterinarian.
  • Excessive vocalization: Some cats meow frequently when adjusting to a new environment. This usually decreases as they settle in.

When to Call for Help

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue provides post-adoption support because we want every placement to succeed. Do not hesitate to contact us if your cat is not eating after two days, seems lethargic or unwell, or if you are struggling with a behavioral issue. Many challenges in the first week are solvable with a little guidance from someone who knows your cat's history. Our foster families are often your best resource during this transition.

Remember: the first week is not representative of your future together. The cat hiding under your bed right now may be sleeping on your pillow within a month. Give it time.
