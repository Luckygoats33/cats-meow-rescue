Understanding and Managing Cat Aggression

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · Milwaukie, Oregon

Cat aggression is one of the most common behavior complaints, but it's rarely random. Understanding the type of aggression helps you address the root cause rather than just managing symptoms.

Play Aggression

The most common type, especially in kittens and young cats. Signs: stalking your ankles, pouncing on hands and feet, biting during play. The fix: never use your hands as toys. Redirect with wand toys and interactive play sessions. If your cat bites during petting, they've likely become overstimulated — learn their limit and stop petting before they reach it.

Fear Aggression

A frightened cat may lash out when cornered. Signs: ears flat, body low, dilated pupils, hissing. The fix: give the cat an escape route. Never corner a scared cat. Address the source of fear and create safe hiding spots. Shy cats are more prone to fear aggression.

Redirected Aggression

A cat aroused by something they can't reach (a cat outside the window, a loud noise) may redirect that arousal toward the nearest available target — usually you or another pet. The fix: block visual access to triggers, separate agitated cats for a cooling-off period (hours, not minutes), and reintroduce slowly.

Inter-Cat Aggression

Common in multi-cat households, especially with improper introductions. Signs: staring, blocking pathways, fighting. The fix: ensure adequate resources (litter boxes, food stations, resting spots), provide vertical space, and consider a full re-introduction protocol if conflicts are severe.

When to Get Professional Help

If aggression is severe, sudden, or causing injury, consult your vet (to rule out pain or illness) and a certified animal behaviorist. The American College of Veterinary Behaviorists can help you find a qualified professional.
