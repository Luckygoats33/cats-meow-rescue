Fostering Bottle Baby Kittens: A Complete Guide

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 14, 2026

What Are Bottle Babies?

Bottle baby kittens are neonatal kittens (typically under 4 weeks old) who cannot eat on their own and must be bottle-fed a kitten milk replacer (KMR). They are usually orphans whose mother has died, abandoned them, or cannot produce enough milk. These are the most vulnerable cats that enter rescue, and without round-the-clock care, they will not survive.

Feeding Schedule

Neonatal kittens need to be fed every 2-3 hours around the clock for the first two weeks, gradually extending to every 3-4 hours by week three. Use a specialized kitten bottle or syringe with KMR (never cow's milk). Always feed kittens on their stomachs, never on their backs like human babies, to prevent aspiration. Weigh kittens daily to ensure they are gaining weight.

Stimulation and Warmth

Kittens under 3-4 weeks cannot urinate or defecate on their own. After each feeding, gently stimulate the genital area with a warm, damp cotton ball to trigger elimination. Kittens also cannot regulate their body temperature, so a heating pad set on low under half the bedding (so they can move away if too warm) is essential.

The Reward

Bottle feeding is exhausting work, but it is also the most rewarding form of fostering. You literally save a life that would be lost without your intervention. Watching a tiny, helpless kitten grow into a healthy, playful cat ready for adoption is an extraordinary experience. Cat's Meow Cat Rescue provides training, supplies, and 24/7 support for bottle baby fosters.
