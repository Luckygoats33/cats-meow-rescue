Moving with Cats in Portland: A Stress-Free Guide

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 6, 2026

Before the Move

Keep your cat's routine as normal as possible during packing. Designate one room as the cat's safe space and pack it last. Place the carrier out early so it becomes familiar. Update your cat's microchip registration and ID tags with your new address before moving day. If moving within the Portland metro area, try to visit the new home with your cat's bedding to pre-scent the space.

Moving Day

On moving day, confine your cat to their safe room with food, water, litter, and a note on the door reminding movers not to open it. Alternatively, board your cat or have them stay with a friend for the day. Moving day is chaotic with open doors and loud noises, both of which can cause a panicked cat to escape.

At the New Home

Set up one room first with all of your cat's familiar items: litter box, food, water, bedding, and toys. Keep the cat confined to this room for the first few days while they adjust to the new smells and sounds. Gradually allow access to additional rooms one at a time. Most cats adjust to a new home within 1-2 weeks.

Portland-Specific Tips

If moving to or within Portland, check that your new home's windows have secure screens (Portland breezes will tempt cats to investigate). Verify that the new neighborhood does not have specific hazards (busy roads, known coyote areas). Update your address with your veterinarian and with your cat's microchip registry. If your new home is near McLoughlin, 82nd, or other busy corridors, indoor-only is especially important.
