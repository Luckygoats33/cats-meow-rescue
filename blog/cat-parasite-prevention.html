Flea, Tick, and Worm Prevention for Oregon Cats

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · Milwaukie, Oregon

Oregon's mild, wet climate makes it a haven for fleas, ticks, and intestinal parasites year-round. Even indoor-only cats need parasite prevention — fleas hitch rides on clothing and other pets, and parasites can enter through open windows.

Fleas: The #1 Parasite

Fleas cause itching, skin allergies, tapeworms, and anemia in severe cases. A single flea can lay 50 eggs per day, leading to rapid infestations. Year-round topical or oral flea prevention is essential in Oregon. Our favorites are prescription products like Revolution Plus and Bravecto — they're more effective than over-the-counter options.

Ticks in Oregon

Western black-legged ticks (which can carry Lyme disease) are found throughout the Willamette Valley and Coast Range. If your cat has any outdoor access — even a catio — tick prevention is important. Many combination products cover both fleas and ticks.

Intestinal Worms

Roundworms, hookworms, and tapeworms are common in cats. Cats can get roundworms from contaminated soil tracked in on shoes, tapeworms from ingesting fleas, and hookworms from contaminated environments. Annual fecal tests and regular deworming (your vet will recommend a schedule) keep these under control.

What Cat's Meow Provides

Every cat adopted from our rescue is dewormed and flea-treated before going home. We'll let you know what products were used and when the next dose is due. Talk to your vet about a long-term prevention plan appropriate for your cat's lifestyle.

Warning: Never Use Dog Products on Cats

Some dog flea products contain permethrin, which is toxic and potentially fatal to cats. Always use cat-specific products and consult your vet before starting any prevention regimen.

For Oregon-specific parasite information, the Oregon State University College of Veterinary Medicine provides regional parasite maps and prevention advice.
