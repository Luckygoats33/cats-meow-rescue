Traveling with Your Cat: Tips for Stress-Free Trips

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · March 22, 2025

Most cats are homebodies who prefer the comfort of their own territory, but sometimes travel is unavoidable. Whether you are moving across town, heading to the vet, or taking a longer trip, proper preparation can make the experience much less stressful for both you and your cat. Here are our best tips from the team at Cat's Meow Cat Rescue.

Choosing the Right Carrier

A sturdy, well-ventilated carrier is the single most important piece of travel equipment. Look for these features:

  • Hard-sided carriers offer the best protection in a car and are required by most airlines. Choose one with a top-loading option for easier loading.
  • Size matters: Your cat should be able to stand up, turn around, and lie down comfortably. Too large is not better, as cats feel more secure in a snug space.
  • Familiar scent: Leave the carrier out at home with a soft blanket inside so your cat can explore it on their own terms. Spraying the interior with Feliway (a synthetic calming pheromone) 30 minutes before travel can also help.

Never travel with your cat loose in the car. A frightened cat can dart under the brake pedal, causing an accident, or escape through an opened door.

Car Travel Essentials

For car trips, secure the carrier on the back seat with a seatbelt threaded through the handle. Avoid placing it in the front seat where airbags could be dangerous. Pack these essentials:

  1. A portable litter box and a small bag of your cat's usual litter.
  2. Food, water, and collapsible bowls (withhold food for two hours before departure to reduce nausea).
  3. Any medications your cat takes, plus a copy of their veterinary records.
  4. Paper towels, waste bags, and a spare blanket for cleanup.
  5. A recent photo of your cat in case they escape.

On longer drives, offer water during stops but keep your cat in the carrier. Never leave your cat in a parked car, even with windows cracked. Temperatures inside a car can reach dangerous levels within minutes, even on mild Oregon spring days.

Flying with Your Cat

If you must fly, book a direct flight whenever possible and choose cabin over cargo. Most airlines allow small cats in an approved soft-sided carrier that fits under the seat. Call the airline well in advance to reserve a spot, as most limit the number of pets per flight.

You will need a health certificate from your veterinarian issued within 10 days of travel for domestic flights. International travel has additional requirements including vaccinations, microchip verification, and sometimes quarantine periods. Start planning international trips at least two to three months ahead.

Reducing Travel Anxiety

Cats show stress through vocalizing, panting, drooling, or eliminating in the carrier. To minimize anxiety:

  • Take short practice car rides in the weeks leading up to a trip.
  • Cover the carrier with a light blanket to create a den-like feeling.
  • Play calming music or keep the car quiet rather than playing loud radio.
  • Talk to your veterinarian about anti-anxiety medication for particularly stressful trips. Gabapentin is commonly prescribed for travel anxiety in cats and is safe when used as directed.

Hotel and Destination Tips

When staying in a hotel or visiting a new place, set up a small "base camp" for your cat in the bathroom or a quiet corner with their litter box, food, water, and a familiar blanket. Keep windows and exterior doors closed, and hang the "Do Not Disturb" sign so housekeeping does not accidentally let your cat out.

At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we recommend microchipping and keeping ID tags current before any trip. If your cat escapes in an unfamiliar area, a microchip dramatically increases the chances of a reunion. All cats adopted through our rescue are microchipped before going home.
