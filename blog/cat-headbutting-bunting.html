Why Cats Headbutt You: Understanding Bunting Behavior

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 1, 2026

What Is Bunting?

Bunting is when a cat rubs or pushes their head against you, other animals, or objects. Cats have scent glands on their forehead, cheeks, and chin that release pheromones during bunting. This behavior serves multiple purposes: marking you as familiar and safe, expressing trust, and strengthening social bonds.

Why Your Cat Headbutts You

When your cat headbutts you, they are claiming you as part of their social group. It is one of the most affectionate behaviors a cat can display because it requires them to be physically close and vulnerable. Cats reserve bunting for individuals they trust and feel comfortable with.

Responding to Headbutts

The best response to a cat headbutt is a gentle head bump back, chin scratches, or soft verbal acknowledgment. Do not pull away abruptly or ignore the gesture. Your cat is making a deliberate social overture. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, foster parents note which cats are enthusiastic bunters because it indicates strong social bonds and a cat well-suited for an affectionate home.
