How to Trim Your Cat's Nails at Home

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 12, 2026

Why Trim?

Indoor cats do not wear down their nails naturally like outdoor cats do. Overgrown nails can curve and grow into the paw pad, causing pain and infection. Regular trimming every 2-3 weeks prevents this and reduces damage to furniture. It also makes kneading on your lap more comfortable and reduces scratching injuries during play.

Getting Your Cat Used to It

Before attempting to trim, spend several days handling your cat's paws during calm moments. Touch the paws, gently press the pads to extend the nails, and reward with treats. Once your cat tolerates paw handling without resistance, you are ready for the next step. Never force the issue. Patience now saves stress later.

The Trimming Process

Use sharp, cat-specific nail clippers (guillotine-style or scissor-style). Hold the paw gently but firmly, press the pad to extend the nail, and clip only the transparent tip, avoiding the pink quick (the blood vessel inside the nail). If you cannot see the quick, clip only the very tip. It is better to take too little than too much. If you accidentally cut the quick, apply styptic powder to stop the bleeding.

If You Cannot Do It

Some cats will never tolerate nail trimming at home, and that is fine. Most Portland-area veterinary clinics and groomers offer nail trimming for $10-$20. DoveLewis and many mobile vet services also provide this service. There is no shame in getting professional help for nail trimming.
