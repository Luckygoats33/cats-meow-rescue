Why Cats Scratch and How to Redirect the Behavior

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · February 8, 2025

Few things frustrate new cat owners faster than discovering claw marks on a sofa arm or watching their cat shred the corner of a favorite rug. But scratching is not misbehavior — it is one of the most natural and essential activities in a cat's daily life. Understanding why cats scratch is the first step toward redirecting the behavior and protecting your furniture at the same time.

Why Scratching Is Essential

Cats scratch for several important reasons, and none of them involve spite or destructiveness:

  • Claw maintenance: Scratching removes the dead outer sheath of the claws, revealing sharp, healthy nails beneath. This is necessary grooming behavior.
  • Stretching: When a cat reaches up and pulls down against a surface, they are giving their shoulders, legs, and paws a full stretch. Watch a cat wake from a nap — scratching is almost always their first activity.
  • Territory marking: Cats have scent glands in their paw pads. Scratching leaves both a visual mark and a scent marker that communicates their presence to other cats.
  • Stress relief: Scratching feels good. It releases tension and helps cats cope with excitement, anxiety, or changes in their environment.

Choosing the Right Scratching Posts

The number one reason cats ignore scratching posts is that the posts do not meet their preferences. Here is what to look for:

  • Height and stability: A scratching post must be tall enough for your cat to fully extend their body. It must also be sturdy — a wobbly post will be abandoned immediately. Look for posts with a wide, heavy base.
  • Material: Most cats prefer sisal rope or sisal fabric. Some prefer corrugated cardboard or bare wood. Carpet-covered posts can actually encourage carpet scratching elsewhere in the house.
  • Orientation: Some cats prefer vertical scratching, others prefer horizontal. Observe your cat's natural tendencies. If they scratch the side of the couch, they want a vertical surface. If they attack the rug, try a flat cardboard scratcher.

Strategic Placement

Place scratching posts where your cat already wants to scratch. Put one near their favorite napping spot, since cats love to scratch right after waking up. Place another near the piece of furniture they have been targeting. Tucking a scratching post in a back corner where nobody goes is a guaranteed way to have it ignored.

Multiple scratching surfaces throughout the home work better than a single post. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we recommend at least one scratching surface per cat, plus one extra — similar to the litter box rule.

Redirecting Away from Furniture

If your cat is already scratching furniture, use these strategies:

  1. Place a scratching post directly next to the targeted furniture piece.
  2. Make the furniture less appealing with double-sided tape (cats dislike sticky surfaces on their paws) or aluminum foil temporarily.
  3. Encourage use of the post by rubbing it with catnip or dangling a toy near it.
  4. Reward your cat with treats and praise when they use the post.
  5. Once the habit shifts, you can gradually move the post to your preferred location — a few inches per day.

What Not to Do

Never punish a cat for scratching. Yelling, spraying with water, or physical correction will not stop scratching — it will only damage your relationship and increase your cat's stress, which often leads to more scratching. Declawing is an amputation procedure that causes lasting pain and behavioral problems, and many veterinarians in Oregon and nationwide now refuse to perform it.
