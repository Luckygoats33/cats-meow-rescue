Building an Emergency Kit for Your Cat

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · June 20, 2025

Oregon residents know that emergencies can strike with little warning. From wildfires and ice storms to earthquakes and flooding, the Pacific Northwest presents real natural disaster risks. If you have a cat, being prepared means having a plan and supplies ready for them too. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we urge every cat owner to build an emergency kit and practice their evacuation plan before they need it.

Essential Supplies for Your Cat's Emergency Kit

Pack the following items in a waterproof bag or container that you can grab quickly:

  • Food and water: At least five days' supply of your cat's regular food (canned and/or dry) plus bottled water. Include a manual can opener if using canned food.
  • Medications: A minimum two-week supply of any medications your cat takes, stored in a waterproof bag with dosing instructions.
  • Litter and a portable litter box: A small disposable aluminum pan works well. Pack enough litter for five days.
  • Carrier: A sturdy carrier labeled with your name, phone number, and your cat's name. One carrier per cat.
  • Comfort items: A familiar blanket or toy that carries your cat's scent. This helps reduce stress in an unfamiliar environment.
  • Collapsible food and water bowls.
  • Waste bags and paper towels for cleanup.

Important Documents to Include

Keep copies of these documents in a sealed plastic bag inside your kit:

  • Veterinary records: Vaccination history, spay/neuter certificate, and any medical conditions or allergies.
  • Proof of ownership: Adoption papers, registration, or microchip documentation.
  • Recent photos: Clear, current photos of your cat from multiple angles in case you become separated. Include photos of any distinguishing markings.
  • Emergency contacts: Your veterinarian's number, the nearest emergency animal hospital, and a trusted friend or family member who can care for your cat if you are unable to.

Tip: Store digital copies of all documents in your phone or a cloud service as a backup.

Creating an Evacuation Plan

An emergency kit is only useful if you can get to it and get out safely. Plan ahead:

  1. Know your evacuation routes. Identify at least two routes out of your neighborhood and practice them.
  2. Identify pet-friendly shelters and hotels. In Oregon, many Red Cross shelters coordinate with local animal services, but not all allow pets directly. Research pet-friendly hotels along your evacuation routes and save their numbers in your phone.
  3. Have a "grab and go" plan for your cat. Know where your cat tends to hide when scared (under the bed, in a closet) so you can find them quickly. Practice getting your cat into the carrier calmly.
  4. Never leave your cat behind. If it is not safe for you to stay, it is not safe for your cat either.

Oregon-Specific Emergency Resources

Oregon cat owners should be aware of these resources:

  • Oregon Humane Society operates emergency sheltering programs during major disasters.
  • Clackamas County Emergency Management provides alerts and evacuation zone information for our local area.
  • Oregon Department of Emergency Management offers disaster preparedness guides that include pet planning at oregon.gov/oem.
  • Sign up for Clackamas County public alerts to receive evacuation notices for Milwaukie, Gladstone, and surrounding areas.

Maintaining Your Kit

Building the kit is the first step. Keeping it current is equally important. Check your emergency kit every six months: rotate food and water to keep them fresh, update medications, replace outgrown carriers for kittens, and make sure all documents and photos are current.

At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we have helped coordinate emergency response for animals during Oregon wildfire seasons, and we know that preparedness saves lives. Every cat deserves an owner who is ready to protect them when disaster strikes. Take an hour this weekend to build your kit. You will be glad you did.
