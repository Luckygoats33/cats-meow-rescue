Cat Grass and Catnip: Safe Plants for Your Cat

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 3, 2026

Cat Grass

Cat grass (typically wheatgrass, oat grass, or barley grass) is safe for cats to eat and provides fiber, aids digestion, and satisfies the instinct to chew on vegetation. Growing cat grass indoors gives your cat a safe alternative to potentially toxic houseplants. It grows easily in Portland's climate, even indoors, and can be started from seed in a shallow container with potting soil.

Catnip

Catnip (Nepeta cataria) contains nepetalactone, which triggers a euphoric response in about 50-70% of cats. The effect lasts 5-15 minutes and is completely safe. Catnip sensitivity is genetic and does not develop until 3-6 months of age. Dried catnip can be sprinkled on toys, scratching posts, and bedding. Fresh catnip plants grow prolifically in Portland gardens.

Silver Vine and Valerian

For cats that do not respond to catnip, silver vine (Actinidia polygama) is an alternative that affects approximately 80% of cats. Valerian root also triggers a catnip-like response in many cats. Both are safe and available at Portland-area pet stores and online.

Toxic Plants to Avoid

Many common houseplants and Portland garden plants are toxic to cats. Lilies (all parts, including pollen), poinsettias, dieffenbachia, pothos, philodendrons, sago palms, and tulip bulbs are all dangerous. The ASPCA maintains a comprehensive list of toxic and non-toxic plants. If you have a cat, research every plant in your home and garden to ensure safety.
