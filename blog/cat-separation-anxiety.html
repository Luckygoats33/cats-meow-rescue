Cat Separation Anxiety: Signs and Solutions

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 2, 2026

Cats Can Be Clingy Too

Contrary to the stereotype that cats are aloof and independent, many cats form deep attachments to their owners and experience genuine distress when left alone. Separation anxiety in cats is increasingly recognized by veterinary behaviorists. Rescue cats who have experienced abandonment or multiple rehomings may be particularly susceptible.

Signs of Separation Anxiety

Common signs include excessive vocalization when you prepare to leave, destructive behavior while you are away, inappropriate elimination (especially on your clothes or bed), excessive grooming leading to hair loss, not eating when alone, and overly enthusiastic greetings when you return. Security cameras can help you observe your cat's behavior while you are away.

Solutions That Work

Provide environmental enrichment to keep your cat occupied: puzzle feeders, window perches with bird feeders outside, interactive toys on timers, and cat TV (YouTube videos for cats). Desensitize your departure routine by picking up keys and putting on shoes without leaving. Consider a second cat for companionship. In severe cases, veterinary behaviorists may recommend anti-anxiety medication.

Creating a Routine

Cats thrive on routine. Maintain consistent feeding times, play sessions before you leave, and a calm departure and arrival routine. Leave a piece of your worn clothing in the cat's favorite spot so they can be comforted by your scent. These simple measures can make a significant difference for a cat with separation anxiety.
