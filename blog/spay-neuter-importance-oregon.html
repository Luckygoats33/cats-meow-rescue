Spay and Neuter: Why It's Critical for Oregon Cats

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 9, 2026

Health Benefits of Spaying and Neutering

Spaying female cats eliminates the risk of ovarian and uterine cancer and greatly reduces the risk of mammary cancer if done before the first heat cycle. Neutering male cats eliminates testicular cancer risk, reduces spraying behavior, and decreases the urge to roam and fight. Both surgeries reduce the risk of FIV transmission, which spreads primarily through bite wounds during fights between intact males.

The Overpopulation Crisis in Oregon

A single unspayed female cat can produce up to 12 kittens per year. In five years, one pair of cats and their offspring can theoretically produce over 11,000 cats. Oregon's shelters and rescues are already overwhelmed, especially during kitten season. Cat's Meow Cat Rescue takes in as many cats as our foster network can support, but we cannot keep pace without community-wide spay and neuter efforts.

Low-Cost Options in the Portland Area

Several organizations offer low-cost or free spay/neuter services in the Portland metro area. The Feral Cat Coalition of Oregon provides free services for community cats. Oregon Humane Society, Multnomah County Animal Services, and various mobile clinics offer subsidized surgeries. Cat's Meow Cat Rescue ensures every cat is spayed or neutered before adoption.

When to Spay or Neuter

Most veterinarians recommend spaying or neutering at 4-6 months of age, before sexual maturity. Pediatric spay/neuter (as early as 8 weeks) is also safe and increasingly common in shelter medicine. All Cat's Meow cats are altered before adoption regardless of age.
