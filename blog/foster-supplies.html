Essential Supplies for Cat Foster Parents

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · January 28, 2025

One of the most common questions we hear from new foster parents is, "What do I need to have ready before the cat arrives?" The good news is that Cat's Meow Cat Rescue provides many essentials, but having your space properly prepared makes the transition smoother for both you and your new foster cat. Here is a practical breakdown of what the rescue supplies and what you should have on hand.

What Cat's Meow Provides

We want fostering to be financially accessible, so Cat's Meow covers the core necessities for every foster placement:

  • Cat food: Dry and wet food appropriate for your foster cat's age and health needs
  • Litter: Clumping clay litter in sufficient quantity for the foster period
  • Veterinary care: All medical expenses including vaccinations, spay/neuter, medications, and emergency visits
  • Carrier: A loaner carrier for vet visits and transport if you do not have one
  • Medications: Any prescribed treatments with clear dosing instructions

If you prefer to use a specific brand of food or litter, you are welcome to do so. Some foster parents find that their foster cats do better on a particular diet, and our coordinators are always happy to discuss nutrition options.

Setting Up Your Foster Room

The most important thing you can prepare is a dedicated space where your foster cat can decompress. A spare bedroom, bathroom, or large walk-in closet works well. This room should include:

  • A litter box placed away from food and water
  • Food and water bowls (ceramic or stainless steel are easiest to sanitize)
  • A cozy hiding spot — a cardboard box with a blanket draped over it works perfectly
  • A soft bed or folded towels for sleeping
  • A scratching post or cardboard scratcher

Oregon weather can mean chilly rooms, especially in older homes. Make sure the foster space stays comfortably warm, particularly for kittens or cats recovering from surgery.

Helpful Extras to Have on Hand

While not strictly required, these items make the fostering experience easier and more enjoyable:

  • Baby gate: Useful for keeping foster cats separated from resident pets while allowing visual introduction
  • Enzyme-based cleaner: Essential for accidents — regular cleaners will not fully eliminate odors that attract repeat marking
  • Digital kitchen scale: Critical for monitoring kitten weight gain
  • Interactive toys: Wand toys and crinkle balls help with socialization and exercise
  • Nail clippers: Basic cat nail trimmers for routine maintenance
  • Lint roller: Your new best friend, especially with long-haired fosters

Supplies for Special Situations

Some foster placements require additional supplies depending on the cat's needs:

  • Bottle baby kittens: Kitten milk replacer, bottles, nipples, heating pad, and a kitchen scale (Cat's Meow supplies these)
  • Medical fosters: Pill pockets, syringes for liquid medication, and possibly an e-collar (all provided by the rescue)
  • Feral or shy cats: Extra hiding spots, Feliway diffusers, and patience — no special equipment needed beyond what you would normally set up

Where to Find Affordable Supplies

If you want to supplement what Cat's Meow provides, there are several budget-friendly options in the Milwaukie and Gladstone area. Thrift stores often have bowls and blankets at low cost. Dollar stores carry basic cleaning supplies and storage bins that work as litter boxes for kittens. Our local PetSmart partners sometimes offer discounts to verified foster parents as well.

The bottom line: do not let supply concerns hold you back from fostering. Cat's Meow Cat Rescue is committed to making sure you have everything you need. Reach out to your foster coordinator if you are missing anything, and we will get it to you.
