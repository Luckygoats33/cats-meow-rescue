Plants Toxic to Cats: A Guide for Oregon Plant Lovers

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · June 2, 2025

Oregonians love their plants. From lush indoor jungles to carefully tended gardens, greenery is a way of life in the Pacific Northwest. But if you share your home with a cat, you need to know which plants could put them in danger. Many popular houseplants and garden favorites are toxic to cats, and even a small nibble can cause serious illness. Here is what every Oregon cat owner should know.

The Most Dangerous: Lilies

Lilies are the number one plant threat to cats. All parts of true lilies (genus Lilium and Hemerocallis) are extremely toxic, including the pollen, petals, leaves, and even the water in the vase. Ingesting even a tiny amount can cause acute kidney failure within 24 to 72 hours, which is often fatal without immediate veterinary treatment.

Dangerous lily species include Easter lilies, tiger lilies, Asiatic lilies, Japanese show lilies, and daylilies. If you have a cat, do not keep these plants in your home or garden, period. If you suspect your cat has come into contact with any part of a lily, seek emergency veterinary care immediately. Time is critical.

Common Toxic Houseplants

Many popular indoor plants can harm cats if ingested:

  • Pothos (Devil's Ivy): Causes oral irritation, drooling, vomiting, and difficulty swallowing.
  • Dieffenbachia (Dumb Cane): Contains calcium oxalate crystals that cause intense mouth and throat burning.
  • Philodendron: Similar effects to pothos, with oral pain and gastrointestinal distress.
  • Sago Palm: Highly toxic. All parts, especially the seeds, can cause liver failure and death.
  • Snake Plant (Sansevieria): Causes nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea if chewed.
  • Aloe Vera: The gel is mildly toxic to cats, causing vomiting, diarrhea, and lethargy.
  • Jade Plant: Ingestion can cause vomiting, depression, and incoordination.

Oregon Garden Plants to Watch

Several plants common in Oregon landscapes and gardens are also hazardous:

  • Rhododendrons and Azaleas: Oregon's state flower is toxic to cats. Ingestion can cause vomiting, diarrhea, weakness, and in severe cases, cardiac failure.
  • Tulips and Daffodils: The bulbs are the most toxic part. Spring planting season is a particularly risky time if your cat has any outdoor access.
  • Foxglove: Contains cardiac glycosides that can cause fatal heart rhythm changes.
  • Oleander: Every part of this plant is highly toxic and can cause death.
  • Autumn Crocus: Causes severe vomiting, organ damage, and bone marrow suppression.

Cat-Safe Plant Alternatives

You do not have to give up plants entirely. Plenty of beautiful options are non-toxic to cats:

  • Spider Plant: Hardy, easy to grow, and completely safe for cats (though they love to chew it).
  • Boston Fern: A lush, pet-safe choice that thrives in Oregon's humidity.
  • African Violet: Adds color without risk.
  • Calathea: Stunning foliage and non-toxic.
  • Cat Grass (wheatgrass or oat grass): Provide this as a safe chewing option to redirect your cat away from other plants.
  • Catnip and Catmint: Safe, and most cats love them.

What to Do If Your Cat Eats a Toxic Plant

If you catch your cat chewing on a toxic plant or notice symptoms like drooling, vomiting, lethargy, or loss of appetite, act quickly:

  1. Remove any plant material from your cat's mouth and fur.
  2. Identify the plant if possible and take a sample or photo.
  3. Call your veterinarian or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435 (a consultation fee applies).
  4. Do not induce vomiting unless specifically instructed by a veterinarian.

At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we remind all adopters to check their homes for toxic plants before bringing a new cat home. The ASPCA maintains a comprehensive list of toxic and non-toxic plants at aspca.org that we recommend bookmarking. A few minutes of plant-checking can prevent a life-threatening emergency.
