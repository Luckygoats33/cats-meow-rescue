Why Do Cats Purr? It's Not Always What You Think

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 11, 2026

The Mechanics of Purring

Cats purr by rapidly contracting and relaxing the muscles of their larynx (voice box), creating vibrations at a frequency of 25-150 Hz. This happens during both inhalation and exhalation, which is why purring sounds continuous. Domestic cats are the only felines that can purr continuously, and the mechanism is so efficient that it requires very little energy.

Purring Does Not Always Mean Happy

While cats frequently purr when content, they also purr when stressed, sick, injured, or in pain. Cats have been observed purring while at the veterinarian, during labor, and even while dying. Scientists believe cats may purr in these situations as a self-soothing mechanism, similar to how humans might hum when nervous.

Healing Properties of Purring

Research suggests that the specific frequencies of cat purring (25-50 Hz) can promote bone density, reduce inflammation, and accelerate healing. Some scientists theorize that cats evolved purring as a low-energy way to maintain bone and tissue health during their long periods of rest and sleep. Studies in human medicine have explored the therapeutic effects of these frequencies.

Reading Your Cat's Purr in Context

To understand what your cat's purr means, look at the full picture. A relaxed cat with soft eyes, kneading paws, and a loose body posture is purring from contentment. A cat purring while hunched, hiding, or with dilated pupils may be purring to self-soothe through pain or stress. Context is everything when interpreting feline communication.
