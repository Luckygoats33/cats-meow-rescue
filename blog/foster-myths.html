Common Myths About Fostering Cats — Debunked

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · February 22, 2025

Fostering cats saves lives, yet many people who would be wonderful foster parents never sign up because of misconceptions about what fostering involves. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we have heard every objection and concern imaginable. Let us set the record straight on the most common myths so you can make an informed decision about whether fostering is right for you.

Myth 1: "I'll Get Too Attached and Want to Keep Every Cat"

This is the number one concern we hear, and it is completely understandable. The truth is that you will get attached, and that is the whole point. A foster cat who bonds with their caregiver is a cat who has learned to trust humans, and that bond is what makes them adoptable. Yes, saying goodbye is emotional. But experienced fosters learn to channel that attachment into motivation: every cat who leaves your home for a forever family opens a spot for the next cat who needs saving.

And if you truly cannot let go of a particular cat? That is okay too. The occasional "foster fail" is a celebrated tradition in rescue. But most foster parents find that the joy of helping multiple cats outweighs the difficulty of individual goodbyes.

Myth 2: "Fostering Is Expensive"

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue covers all veterinary costs, provides food and litter, and can supply basic equipment like carriers, bowls, and bedding. Your out-of-pocket cost is essentially zero unless you choose to purchase extras. We designed our program this way specifically because we believe financial barriers should never prevent someone from fostering.

Myth 3: "I Don't Have Enough Space"

You do not need a sprawling house to foster a cat. A single spare room, a large bathroom, or even a partitioned section of a studio apartment can work. In fact, smaller spaces are often better for newly arrived cats because they feel less overwhelmed. Kittens can be set up in a large dog crate or playpen within a room you already use. Some of our most successful foster parents live in modest apartments throughout the Portland metro area.

Myth 4: "My Own Cats Won't Handle It"

Foster cats are always kept in a separate room initially, with no direct contact with your resident pets. Proper introductions happen gradually over days or weeks, if at all. Many foster parents keep their fosters completely separated for the entire foster period with no issues. Your resident cats' safety and comfort always come first, and Cat's Meow coordinators can advise you on managing multi-cat households.

That said, some resident cats are genuinely not suited to having fosters in the home. If your cat becomes deeply stressed by the scent of another cat behind a closed door, fostering may not be the right fit — and that is perfectly fine. There are other ways to help the rescue.

Myth 5: "I Don't Have Enough Experience"

Everyone starts somewhere. Cat's Meow provides orientation and training for all new foster parents, and we match your first placement with a straightforward, healthy cat who does not require specialized care. If you can provide food, water, a clean litter box, and daily affection, you have the skills to foster. Our coordinators are always available for questions, and we have a community of experienced fosters who are happy to mentor newcomers.

Myth 6: "It Takes Too Much Time"

A healthy adult foster cat requires about the same time commitment as a pet cat: feeding twice daily, scooping the litter box, and spending some quality time together. Kittens and medical fosters require more attention, but even bottle babies only need intensive care for a few weeks. Many of our foster parents work full-time jobs and foster successfully by planning their schedules around feeding times.

The Reality of Fostering

Fostering is not always easy, but it is always meaningful. Every cat you foster is a cat who might not have survived without your help. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we have placed hundreds of cats through our foster program, and every one of those success stories started with a volunteer who decided to try despite their doubts. If you have been on the fence, we encourage you to reach out. You might just discover that fostering is one of the most rewarding things you have ever done.
