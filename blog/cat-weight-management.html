Cat Weight Management: Keeping Your Kitty Fit

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · May 28, 2025

Feline obesity is an epidemic. The Association for Pet Obesity Prevention estimates that over 60 percent of cats in the United States are overweight or obese. Those extra pounds are not just cosmetic — they significantly increase your cat's risk of diabetes, joint disease, urinary problems, liver disease, and a shortened lifespan. The good news is that with thoughtful management, you can help your cat reach and maintain a healthy weight.

How to Tell If Your Cat Is Overweight

Most cat owners underestimate their cat's weight because a plump cat has become so normalized. Use the body condition score system to assess your cat:

  • Ribs: You should be able to feel your cat's ribs easily with light pressure. If you have to press firmly through a layer of fat, your cat is likely overweight.
  • Waist: When viewed from above, your cat should have a visible waist — a slight narrowing behind the ribs before the hips.
  • Belly: From the side, the abdomen should tuck up slightly rather than hanging down in a pronounced pouch.
  • Overall shape: A healthy cat has a sleek, proportional shape. An overweight cat appears round or barrel-shaped from above.

Your veterinarian can give you a precise body condition score and recommend an ideal target weight for your individual cat.

Why Cats Gain Weight

Understanding the causes of weight gain helps you address the root problem. Common contributing factors include:

  • Free-feeding — Leaving dry food available at all times leads many cats to overeat out of boredom rather than hunger
  • Calorie-dense food — Some cat foods pack far more calories per cup than others, and portion sizes printed on bags are often more generous than most indoor cats need
  • Lack of exercise — Indoor cats without adequate play and enrichment burn fewer calories than their bodies take in
  • Spaying and neutering — Metabolic changes after sterilization reduce calorie needs by about 25 to 30 percent, but many owners do not adjust portions accordingly
  • Treats and table food — Treats should make up no more than 10 percent of daily calories

Safe Weight Loss Strategies

Cats must lose weight gradually. Rapid weight loss can trigger hepatic lipidosis (fatty liver disease), a life-threatening condition. Aim for a loss of no more than 1 to 2 percent of body weight per week. Work with your veterinarian to calculate a safe daily calorie target and follow these strategies:

  1. Switch to measured meals. Feed two or three portioned meals per day rather than free-feeding. Use a kitchen scale for the most accurate portions.
  2. Choose a high-protein, low-carbohydrate food. Wet food is often ideal because it is lower in carbs and more satiating than dry food.
  3. Increase activity. Add interactive play sessions, puzzle feeders, and vertical climbing spaces to boost daily calorie burn.
  4. Cut back on treats. Replace high-calorie commercial treats with small pieces of cooked plain chicken, or use a portion of their daily kibble allowance as treats.
  5. Weigh regularly. Monthly weigh-ins help you track progress and adjust the plan as needed. Many vet offices in the Portland area offer free weight checks with a technician.

Multi-Cat Household Challenges

Managing one cat's weight in a home with multiple cats can be tricky, especially when one cat needs to lose weight while others are at a healthy size. Solutions include feeding cats in separate rooms, using microchip-activated feeders that only open for a specific cat, and picking up food bowls after a set mealtime rather than leaving them out.

A Lifetime Commitment

Weight management is not a short-term diet — it is an ongoing lifestyle. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we assess every cat's body condition and share dietary recommendations with adopters. If your adopted cat needs to lose a few ounces, we will help you create a plan. A lean, active cat is a healthier cat, and every pound lost adds quality time to the years you spend together.
