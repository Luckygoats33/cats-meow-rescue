Cat Dental Care: Why It Matters and How to Help

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · May 10, 2025

Dental disease is one of the most common health problems in cats, yet it is also one of the most overlooked. Studies estimate that over 70 percent of cats show signs of dental disease by age three. Left untreated, dental problems cause chronic pain, difficulty eating, and can lead to infections that spread to the heart, kidneys, and liver. The good news is that with proper care, most dental issues are preventable or manageable.

Common Dental Problems in Cats

Several dental conditions affect cats, and knowing what to look for helps you catch problems early:

  • Periodontal disease — The most prevalent dental condition, caused by plaque and tartar buildup along the gum line. It progresses from mild gingivitis (red, swollen gums) to severe periodontitis, which can destroy the tissues supporting the teeth and lead to tooth loss.
  • Tooth resorption — A painful condition where the tooth structure breaks down from the inside. It affects an estimated 30 to 70 percent of cats and often requires extraction of the affected teeth.
  • Stomatitis — Severe, painful inflammation of the mouth and gums that can make eating extremely difficult. It sometimes requires extraction of most or all teeth, after which most cats actually eat more comfortably.
  • Fractured teeth — Broken teeth can expose the sensitive pulp inside, causing pain and infection. Cats who chew on hard objects or experience trauma are most at risk.

Signs Your Cat May Have Dental Problems

Cats rarely stop eating entirely, even when they are in significant oral pain. Instead, watch for these more subtle indicators:

  • Bad breath that persists beyond what is normal
  • Drooling or dropping food while eating
  • Favoring one side of the mouth while chewing
  • Pawing at the face or mouth
  • Red, swollen, or bleeding gums
  • Reluctance to eat dry food or hard treats
  • Weight loss or decreased appetite
  • Behavioral changes such as increased irritability or hiding

Professional Dental Care

A professional dental cleaning performed by your veterinarian under general anesthesia is the gold standard for feline dental care. During the procedure, the vet team removes plaque and tartar from all tooth surfaces (including below the gum line where problems often hide), takes dental X-rays to evaluate the roots and jawbone, and extracts any teeth that are damaged beyond repair.

Anesthesia is essential for a thorough and safe dental cleaning. "Anesthesia-free" dental procedures cannot address problems below the gum line and are stressful and potentially dangerous for cats. Most veterinarians in the Milwaukie and Gladstone area offer comprehensive dental services and can advise you on how often your cat needs professional cleanings.

Home Dental Care

Between professional cleanings, you can support your cat's dental health at home:

  • Brushing — Daily tooth brushing with a cat-specific toothpaste is the most effective home care. Never use human toothpaste, as fluoride and xylitol are toxic to cats. Start slowly by letting your cat taste the toothpaste, then progress to rubbing a finger brush along the gum line.
  • Dental treats and diets — Products with the VOHC (Veterinary Oral Health Council) seal have been proven to reduce plaque and tartar. Look for the seal on treats, chews, and dental-specific dry foods.
  • Water additives — Some enzymatic water additives help reduce bacteria in the mouth, though they are less effective than brushing.
  • Regular inspections — Gently lift your cat's lips weekly to check for redness, swelling, or any visible abnormalities along the gum line.

Dental Health at Cat's Meow Cat Rescue

At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we assess every cat's dental health as part of our intake and pre-adoption process. Cats with significant dental issues receive treatment before being placed for adoption whenever possible. When you adopt from us, we share any dental findings or recommendations so you and your veterinarian can plan ongoing care. A healthy mouth means a healthier, happier cat — and that is what we want for every one of our alumni.
