Cat-Proofing Your Home: Safety Tips for New Cat Owners

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · February 8, 2025

Bringing a new cat home is exciting, but before your feline friend starts exploring, it is essential to make your space safe. Cats are curious, agile, and surprisingly resourceful at getting into things they shouldn't. Whether you are adopting a kitten or an adult cat from Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, a little preparation goes a long way toward preventing accidents and keeping your new companion safe.

Secure Windows and Balconies

Cats love perching on windowsills and watching the world go by, but open windows without secure screens pose a serious fall risk. High-rise syndrome is a real veterinary concern, and even cats on the second floor can sustain injuries from falls.

  • Install sturdy window screens that lock in place and cannot be pushed out.
  • If you have a balcony, consider cat-safe netting or keep the door closed.
  • Check that all screen doors latch securely so your cat cannot slip outside.

Hide Cords and Small Objects

Electrical cords are irresistible to many cats, especially kittens. Chewing on cords can cause burns, electrical shock, or worse. Use cord covers, cable management channels, or bitter-tasting deterrent sprays to protect both your cat and your electronics.

Small objects like hair ties, rubber bands, buttons, coins, and sewing needles are common culprits in feline foreign body ingestions. Keep these items stored in closed containers or drawers. String, yarn, tinsel, and ribbon are particularly dangerous because they can cause linear foreign body obstructions that require emergency surgery.

Lock Away Toxic Substances

Many common household products are toxic to cats. Store the following securely out of reach:

  • Cleaning products: bleach, disinfectants, laundry pods, and dryer sheets.
  • Medications: acetaminophen (Tylenol), ibuprofen, and prescription drugs. Even a single pill can be fatal.
  • Essential oils: tea tree, eucalyptus, peppermint, and citrus oils are toxic to cats even in diffused form.
  • Pest control products: rodent bait, insecticides, and slug pellets.

Use child-proof locks on cabinets under sinks and in bathrooms where these products are typically stored.

Check Your Furniture and Appliances

Cats squeeze into surprisingly small spaces. Before running your dryer, always check inside the drum. Warm laundry fresh from the dryer is a cat magnet, and tragically, cats have been injured in running machines. Similarly, check recliners and sofa beds before closing them, as cats love to hide in the mechanisms underneath.

Secure tall bookshelves and dressers to the wall with anti-tip straps. A climbing cat can topple unsecured furniture, injuring themselves or others. Keep heavy objects away from shelf edges where a curious paw might knock them off.

Create Safe Spaces

Cat-proofing is not just about removing hazards. It is also about creating an environment where your cat feels secure. Set up a designated safe room with a litter box, food, water, a scratching post, and a cozy hiding spot. This is especially important during the first few days when your new cat is adjusting to unfamiliar surroundings.
