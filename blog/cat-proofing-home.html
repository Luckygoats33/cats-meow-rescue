Cat-Proofing Your Home: Safety Tips for New Cat Owners

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · Milwaukie, Oregon

Cats are curious explorers with a talent for finding trouble. Before bringing a new cat home, walk through your space and address these common hazards.

Kitchen Dangers

Stove tops (especially glass-top stoves that retain heat), sharp utensils, toxic foods (onions, garlic, chocolate, grapes, xylitol), open trash cans, and cleaning supplies under the sink. Use child-proof cabinet locks — cats can open cabinets. Never leave unattended food on counters.

Living Areas

Secure loose electrical cords (cats love chewing them — cord covers or bitter apple spray help), remove or secure breakable items from shelves, ensure bookcases and tall furniture are anchored to walls, and keep small objects (hair ties, rubber bands, small toys) off the floor — these are common causes of intestinal blockages.

Windows and Balconies

Install secure screens on all windows — cats can push through loose screens. Never leave windows open without screens, even on upper floors. "High-rise syndrome" (cats falling from windows and balconies) is a real and serious risk. Balconies should be enclosed with cat-safe netting if your cat has access.

Bathroom Hazards

Keep toilet lids closed (drowning risk for kittens), store medications in closed cabinets, secure trash cans, and keep cleaning products out of reach. Dental floss and string are extremely dangerous if swallowed.

Plants

Many common houseplants are toxic to cats, including lilies (potentially fatal), poinsettias, philodendrons, and aloe vera. Remove or relocate all toxic plants before bringing a cat home.

Garage and Outdoor Access

Antifreeze is extremely toxic and attractive to cats — clean spills immediately. Keep all automotive fluids, pesticides, and rodent poisons inaccessible. Block any gaps where a cat could escape to the outdoors. Read our guide on indoor vs. outdoor cats.

The ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center maintains a comprehensive database of cat hazards.
