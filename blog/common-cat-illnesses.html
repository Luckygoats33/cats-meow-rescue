10 Common Cat Illnesses and Their Warning Signs

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · April 20, 2025

Cats are masters at hiding discomfort. In the wild, showing weakness makes an animal vulnerable to predators, and domestic cats retain this instinct. As a cat owner, learning to recognize the subtle warning signs of illness can mean the difference between catching a problem early and facing a serious health crisis. Here are ten common feline illnesses and the symptoms to watch for.

Upper Respiratory Infections

Signs: Sneezing, nasal discharge, watery or goopy eyes, congestion, reduced appetite, and lethargy. Upper respiratory infections are extremely common in shelter environments and are usually caused by feline herpesvirus or calicivirus. Most cases resolve with supportive care, but kittens, senior cats, and immunocompromised cats may need veterinary intervention including antibiotics for secondary bacterial infections.

Urinary Tract Disease

Signs: Straining in the litter box, frequent trips to the litter box with little output, crying while urinating, blood in urine, and urinating outside the box. Feline Lower Urinary Tract Disease (FLUTD) encompasses several conditions including crystals, bladder stones, and idiopathic cystitis. A male cat who cannot urinate is a life-threatening emergency — seek veterinary care immediately.

Chronic Kidney Disease

Signs: Increased thirst and urination, weight loss, decreased appetite, vomiting, and dull coat. Kidney disease is one of the most common conditions in cats over 10 years old. While it cannot be cured, early detection through blood work allows for dietary management and supportive treatments that can extend quality of life significantly.

Dental Disease

Signs: Bad breath, drooling, difficulty eating, pawing at the mouth, swollen or bleeding gums, and dropping food. By age three, the majority of cats have some degree of dental disease. Regular dental checkups and professional cleanings under anesthesia are the most effective prevention and treatment.

Diabetes

Signs: Excessive thirst, increased urination, increased appetite with weight loss, lethargy, and an unkempt coat. Feline diabetes is manageable with insulin therapy and dietary changes. Overweight cats are at significantly higher risk, making weight management an important preventive measure.

Hyperthyroidism

Signs: Weight loss despite a ravenous appetite, hyperactivity, vomiting, diarrhea, increased thirst, and a rapid heart rate. This condition is caused by an overactive thyroid gland and is most common in cats over 10 years old. Treatment options include medication, radioactive iodine therapy, or surgery.

Gastrointestinal Issues

Signs: Vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, loss of appetite, and abdominal discomfort. While the occasional hairball is normal, frequent vomiting is not. Persistent GI symptoms can indicate food sensitivities, inflammatory bowel disease, parasites, or foreign body ingestion. Consult your vet if symptoms last more than 24 to 48 hours.

Obesity

Signs: Difficulty feeling ribs under the fur, a hanging belly, reluctance to play or jump, and labored breathing after minimal activity. More than half of domestic cats are overweight or obese. Obesity increases the risk of diabetes, joint problems, liver disease, and urinary issues.

Ear Infections and Ear Mites

Signs: Head shaking, ear scratching, dark or waxy discharge from the ears, redness inside the ear canal, and an unpleasant odor. Ear mites are especially common in kittens and are highly contagious between cats. A simple ear swab at your vet's office can identify the cause and guide treatment.

Skin Conditions and Allergies

Signs: Excessive scratching or grooming, bald patches, scabs or sores on the skin, and red or inflamed areas. Cats can develop allergies to fleas, food ingredients, or environmental triggers like pollen and dust mites. Identifying and removing the allergen is key, along with veterinary-guided treatment.

When to Call the Vet

Trust your instincts. If something seems off with your cat, it is always better to call your veterinarian and describe what you are seeing than to wait and see. Emergency situations that require immediate care include difficulty breathing, inability to urinate, seizures, sudden paralysis of the hind legs, suspected poisoning, and trauma. Many veterinary emergency clinics in the Portland metro area are open 24 hours. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we encourage all adopters to keep their vet's phone number and a local emergency clinic number saved in their phone.
