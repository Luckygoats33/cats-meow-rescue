Cat Microchipping: Why It Matters in Oregon

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 10, 2026

How Microchips Work

A microchip is a tiny electronic device (about the size of a grain of rice) implanted under your cat's skin, typically between the shoulder blades. It contains a unique identification number that can be read by a scanner at any veterinary clinic or animal shelter. The chip itself has no battery and lasts the lifetime of the cat. All cats adopted from Cat's Meow Cat Rescue are microchipped before going to their forever homes.

Why Microchipping Is Essential

Collars can break, fall off, or be removed. A microchip is permanent identification that cannot be lost. Studies show that microchipped cats are more than 20 times more likely to be returned to their owners if lost. In Oregon, where outdoor hazards include traffic, weather, and wildlife, having a microchipped cat provides essential peace of mind.

Keeping Your Registration Current

The microchip is only useful if your contact information is up to date. When you adopt from Cat's Meow, we register the chip for you, but you must update your information if you move, change phone numbers, or transfer ownership. Most microchip registries allow free updates online.

Where to Get Microchipped in Portland

If your cat is not yet microchipped, any veterinary clinic in the Portland area can perform the procedure, usually for $25-$50. Oregon Humane Society, Pixie Project, and many mobile vet clinics also offer microchipping, sometimes at reduced cost during community events.
