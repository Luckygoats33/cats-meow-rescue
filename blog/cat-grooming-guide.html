Cat Grooming Guide: Brushing, Bathing, and Nail Trimming

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · January 12, 2025

Grooming is one of the most important aspects of cat care that many owners overlook. While cats are famously fastidious self-groomers, they still need regular help from their humans to stay healthy and comfortable. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we see firsthand how proper grooming habits contribute to a cat's overall well-being, and we want every adopter to feel confident caring for their feline companion.

Brushing: The Foundation of Cat Grooming

Regular brushing removes loose fur, prevents matting, distributes natural oils, and reduces hairballs. How often you brush depends on your cat's coat type:

  • Short-haired cats benefit from brushing once or twice a week with a rubber grooming mitt or bristle brush.
  • Medium-haired cats should be brushed two to three times per week using a slicker brush.
  • Long-haired cats (like Persians or Maine Coons) need daily brushing with a wide-toothed comb followed by a slicker brush to prevent painful mats.

Start by brushing in the direction of hair growth, working from head to tail. Pay special attention to areas prone to matting: behind the ears, under the armpits, and along the belly. If you encounter a mat, hold the fur at the base near the skin and gently work through it with a mat splitter. Never pull or cut mats with scissors, as it is dangerously easy to nick the skin.

Bathing: Less Often Than You Think

Most healthy cats rarely need a bath. Their grooming habits keep them remarkably clean. However, baths may be necessary if your cat gets into something sticky or smelly, has a skin condition requiring medicated shampoo, or is an older cat who can no longer groom effectively.

When a bath is needed, follow these steps:

  1. Use lukewarm water and a cat-specific shampoo (never human products).
  2. Place a rubber mat in the sink or tub for traction.
  3. Wet your cat gently from the neck down, avoiding the face and ears.
  4. Lather the shampoo, rinse thoroughly, and towel-dry immediately.
  5. Keep your cat in a warm room until completely dry.

Tip: If your cat panics during baths, waterless cat shampoo or grooming wipes are excellent alternatives.

Nail Trimming: Keeping Claws in Check

Indoor cats need their nails trimmed every two to four weeks. Overgrown nails can curl into the paw pads, causing pain and infection. Use cat-specific nail clippers or a pet nail grinder, and trim only the transparent tip of the nail. Avoid the pink quick, which contains blood vessels and nerves. If you accidentally nick the quick, apply styptic powder to stop the bleeding.

To make nail trimming easier, get your cat used to having their paws handled from an early age. Gently press each toe to extend the nail, offer treats, and keep sessions short. Many cats tolerate nail trims better when they are relaxed or slightly sleepy.

Ear and Eye Care

Check your cat's ears weekly for wax buildup, redness, or odor, which can indicate infection or ear mites. Clean the outer ear gently with a cotton ball dampened with a vet-approved ear cleaner. Never insert cotton swabs into the ear canal.

For eye care, wipe away any discharge from the corners of the eyes with a damp, soft cloth. Use a separate area of the cloth for each eye to prevent spreading any potential infection. If you notice persistent discharge, cloudiness, or squinting, schedule a vet visit promptly.

When to Seek Professional Help

Some grooming tasks are best left to professionals. If your cat has severe matting, needs a sanitary trim, or becomes aggressive during grooming, a professional groomer experienced with cats can help safely. Your veterinarian can also assist with nail trims and ear cleaning during routine checkups.

At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue in Milwaukie, Oregon, we introduce our foster cats to gentle grooming routines so they arrive in their forever homes already comfortable with being brushed and handled. If you have questions about grooming your newly adopted cat, our volunteers are always happy to share tips and product recommendations.
