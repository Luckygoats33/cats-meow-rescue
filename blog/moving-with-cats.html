Moving to a New Home with Cats: A Step-by-Step Guide

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · May 14, 2025

Moving is stressful for everyone, but it can be especially overwhelming for cats. Cats are territorial animals who draw comfort from familiar surroundings, scents, and routines. A move disrupts all of that at once. With careful planning, though, you can make the transition as smooth as possible. Here is a step-by-step guide from Cat's Meow Cat Rescue.

Before the Move: Preparation Is Everything

Start preparing your cat well before moving day:

  • Get your cat comfortable with their carrier. Leave it open in a common area with a cozy blanket inside for several weeks before the move. Toss treats inside so your cat associates it with good things.
  • Maintain your routine. Keep feeding times, play sessions, and litter box locations consistent as long as possible. Cats pick up on household stress, and a predictable routine provides reassurance.
  • Stock up on supplies. Have enough food, litter, and any medications to last through the transition period without needing to rush to the store in an unfamiliar area.
  • Update your cat's microchip and ID tags with your new address and phone number before you move.

Moving Day: Keep Your Cat Secure

Moving day is chaotic, with doors propped open, strangers coming and going, and furniture being shuffled around. This is the highest-risk time for a cat to escape or get injured.

  1. Set up a "safe room." Before the movers arrive, place your cat in a quiet room (like a bathroom) with their litter box, food, water, toys, and a familiar blanket. Close the door and post a sign that says "Do Not Open - Cat Inside."
  2. Move your cat last. After everything else has been loaded, place your cat in their carrier and transport them yourself. Never put your cat in the moving truck.
  3. Keep the car comfortable. Use climate control, cover the carrier with a light blanket, and play soft music or talk calmly to your cat during the drive.

Arriving at Your New Home

Resist the temptation to let your cat explore the entire house right away. Instead, set up a single room as their new base camp. This room should contain all their essentials: litter box, food, water, scratching post, and familiar-smelling items like their bed or a worn t-shirt of yours.

Let your cat adjust to this room for at least two to three days. Visit frequently to offer affection and play, but let them set the pace. Once your cat is eating, using the litter box, and showing curiosity about the door, you can begin opening access to the rest of the house one room at a time.

Common Post-Move Behaviors

It is normal for cats to display stress behaviors after a move. You may notice:

  • Hiding: Completely normal. Provide hiding spots and do not force interaction. Most cats emerge within a few days.
  • Decreased appetite: A day or two of reduced eating is common. If your cat does not eat for more than 48 hours, contact your veterinarian.
  • Litter box avoidance: Make sure the box is in a quiet, accessible spot. If issues persist, try a different litter type or add a second box.
  • Excessive vocalization: Some cats meow more when stressed. Respond with calm reassurance, not scolding.

Settling In for the Long Term

Most cats fully adjust to a new home within two to four weeks, though timid cats may take longer. Help the process along by re-establishing routines as quickly as possible, using Feliway diffusers in key rooms, and spending extra quality time with your cat during the transition.

If you are moving to the Milwaukie, Gladstone, or greater Portland area and need a new veterinarian, Cat's Meow Cat Rescue can recommend trusted clinics in the area. We also encourage you to keep your cat indoors for at least a month after any move so they fully bond with their new territory before encountering the outdoors.
