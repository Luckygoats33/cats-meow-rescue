FIV and FeLV in Cats: What Every Owner Should Know

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · February 22, 2025

A diagnosis of FIV or FeLV can sound frightening, but understanding what these viruses actually mean for your cat is the first step toward providing excellent care. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we test every cat and believe that FIV-positive and FeLV-positive cats deserve loving homes just as much as any other cat. Here is what you need to know.

What Are FIV and FeLV?

FIV (Feline Immunodeficiency Virus) is a slow-acting virus that gradually weakens a cat's immune system over time. It is sometimes compared to HIV in humans, though it cannot be transmitted to people or dogs. FIV is primarily spread through deep bite wounds, making it most common among unneutered male cats who fight.

FeLV (Feline Leukemia Virus) is a more aggressive retrovirus that affects the immune system and can cause anemia, lymphoma, and other cancers. FeLV spreads through prolonged close contact, including mutual grooming, shared food and water bowls, and from mother to kittens during nursing. It is more easily transmitted than FIV.

How Testing Works

Both viruses are detected through a simple blood test called a SNAP combo test, which most veterinary clinics can run in about 10 minutes. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, every cat is tested before being made available for adoption. It is important to understand that:

  • Kittens under 6 months may carry maternal antibodies that cause a false positive for FIV, so retesting at a later age is sometimes recommended
  • A cat recently exposed to FeLV may not test positive for several weeks, so retesting 30 to 60 days after known exposure is standard practice
  • The SNAP test detects antibodies (FIV) and antigens (FeLV), and confirmatory testing such as IFA or PCR may be recommended in some cases

Living with an FIV-Positive Cat

The good news about FIV is that many positive cats live long, healthy, and comfortable lives. FIV progresses slowly, and some cats never develop symptoms at all. The key to managing FIV is keeping your cat indoors, feeding a high-quality diet, maintaining regular veterinary checkups, and addressing any illnesses promptly before they become serious.

FIV-positive cats can live safely with FIV-negative cats as long as the cats get along and do not fight. Casual contact such as sharing food bowls, grooming, and sleeping together does not efficiently transmit the virus. The primary risk is deep bite wounds.

Living with a FeLV-Positive Cat

FeLV is a more serious diagnosis because the virus is more easily transmitted and tends to progress faster. FeLV-positive cats should live exclusively indoors and ideally in a household with only other FeLV-positive cats to prevent spreading the virus. Regular vet visits every 6 months are recommended to monitor blood counts and watch for early signs of lymphoma or other complications.

Despite the challenges, many FeLV-positive cats enjoy several good years with attentive care. Proper nutrition, stress reduction, and prompt treatment of secondary infections all help support a positive cat's quality of life.

Why These Cats Deserve a Chance

Sadly, FIV and FeLV diagnoses carry a stigma that makes these cats harder to place. At Cat's Meow Cat Rescue, we are committed to educating adopters and advocating for these wonderful animals. A positive test result does not define a cat's personality, capacity for love, or ability to be an incredible companion.

If you are considering adopting an FIV or FeLV-positive cat, our team is here to answer every question and provide ongoing support. These cats have so much love to give, and with the right care, they can thrive in a forever home.
