Estate Planning for Your Cat in Oregon

Cat's Meow Cat Rescue · July 11, 2026

Oregon Pet Trust Law

Oregon Revised Statute 130.185 allows you to create a legally enforceable trust for the care of your pet. This trust can designate a caretaker, provide funds for the cat's care, and specify standards of care that must be followed. Unlike informal arrangements with friends or family, a pet trust is legally binding and can be enforced by a court-appointed trustee.

Practical Steps

Start by identifying 2-3 people who are willing to take your cat if you become incapacitated or pass away. Discuss your cat's needs, routine, and veterinary care with these individuals. Create a written document detailing your cat's feeding schedule, medical history, medications, behavioral needs, and veterinarian contact information.

Including Your Cat in Your Will

Consult with an Oregon estate planning attorney to include your cat in your will or create a standalone pet trust. Specify the designated caretaker, an alternate caretaker, funds for the cat's ongoing care, and what should happen to any remaining funds after the cat's death (typically donated to an animal welfare organization).

Emergency Cards

Carry a wallet card or phone tag that indicates you have a pet at home. Include the name and phone number of your designated emergency caretaker. If you are involved in an accident, first responders and hospital staff need to know that an animal is depending on you at home.
