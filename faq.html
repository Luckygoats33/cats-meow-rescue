Adoption Process

10 questions
Start by browsing our available cats on Petfinder or on our website. When you find a cat you're interested in, fill out our online adoption application. A volunteer will review your application, usually within 48 hours, and reach out to schedule a meet-and-greet with the cat in their foster home. If it's a great match, you'll complete the adoption paperwork and take your new family member home.
Adopters must be at least 21 years old and have valid photo ID. If you rent, we'll need confirmation that your landlord allows cats. We ask that all household members agree to the adoption. We do not adopt cats as outdoor-only pets; all cats must live primarily indoors. We may also ask for a veterinary reference if you have or have had other pets.
Most adoptions are completed within 3 to 7 days from application to bringing your cat home. We review applications within 48 hours, and then schedule a meet-and-greet at a time that works for you. Some adoptions happen faster if the foster home is nearby and everyone's schedules align.
Absolutely! We always arrange a meet-and-greet before finalizing any adoption. Since we're a foster-based rescue, meetings happen at the foster home or, for some of our cats, at the PetSmart adoption center in Milwaukie. This gives you a chance to see the cat's personality in a real home setting.
Yes! We adopt throughout the greater Portland metro area and surrounding Oregon communities. We ask that adopters be willing to travel to the foster home or our PetSmart location for the meet-and-greet. For adopters farther away, we can sometimes arrange a midway meeting point on a case-by-case basis.
Yes, and we encourage it! Some of our cats are listed as bonded pairs and must be adopted together. Bonded pairs often transition more smoothly into a new home because they have each other for comfort. We offer a reduced combined adoption fee for bonded pairs.
We have a lifetime return policy. If at any point you can no longer care for your adopted cat, please contact us and we will take the cat back into our rescue. We never want one of our cats to end up in a shelter or on the street. No judgment -- life happens, and the cat's safety is our priority.
We frequently have kittens available, especially during kitten season (spring through fall). Kittens are spayed or neutered before going to their new homes, and we often adopt kittens in pairs to keep them socialized and entertained. Check our Petfinder page or website for current availability.
We generally do not allow surprise adoptions. The person who will be the primary caretaker needs to be part of the adoption process, including filling out the application and attending the meet-and-greet. This helps ensure the cat goes to a home where everyone is prepared and committed.
Of course! Many of our cats do well with other pets. Each cat's profile notes whether they are good with other cats, dogs, or children. We'll help match you with a cat whose personality fits your household, and our foster families can share detailed insights about how each cat interacts with other animals.

Fees & What's Included

7 questions
Adoption fees are $125 for adult cats (1 year and older) and $150 for kittens (under 1 year). Bonded pairs receive a $50 discount on the combined fee. Senior cats (8 years and older) have a reduced fee of $75. These fees help cover the cost of veterinary care each cat receives.
Every adopted cat comes spayed or neutered, up to date on vaccinations (FVRCP and rabies), microchipped and registered, dewormed, treated for fleas, and FIV/FeLV tested. You'll also receive a small starter kit with a few days of the food your cat has been eating in foster care.
The adoption fee covers all initial medical care. Going forward, you'll be responsible for regular veterinary checkups, food, litter, and any future medical needs. We recommend pet insurance for unexpected health issues and are happy to share affordable vet recommendations in the Portland metro area.
We occasionally run adoption specials during events like Clear the Shelters or our anniversary celebrations. Senior cats (8+) always have a reduced fee of $75. We also partner with PetSmart Charities for sponsored adoption events where fees may be reduced. Follow us on social media or sign up for our newsletter to hear about specials.
Adoption fees are due in full at the time of adoption. We accept cash, check, Venmo, and PayPal. If cost is a barrier, please talk to us -- we may be able to connect you with resources or let you know about upcoming fee-waived events. Our priority is finding great homes for our cats.
Adoption fees help offset the significant veterinary costs each cat incurs while in our care. On average, we spend $250-$400 per cat on spay/neuter surgery, vaccinations, microchipping, FIV/FeLV testing, deworming, and flea treatment. The adoption fee covers only a portion of these costs -- the rest is funded by donations and fundraising.
We provide a 14-day health guarantee from the date of adoption. If your newly adopted cat shows signs of illness within this period, we will cover or reimburse reasonable veterinary costs related to pre-existing conditions. We ask that you take your cat to a vet within the first week for a wellness check.

Fostering

8 questions
Fostering means temporarily caring for a cat in your home until they find their forever family. As a foster-based rescue, Cat's Meow relies entirely on foster homes -- we have no physical shelter. Foster parents provide love, socialization, and a safe space while we handle all veterinary care, food, litter, and supplies.
It varies! Kittens may need fostering for 4-8 weeks until they're old enough for spay/neuter and adoption. Adult cats average 2-6 weeks, though some cats with special needs or shy personalities may need a longer stay. We work with you to find a timeline that fits your life, and you can always choose your comfort level.
Yes! We provide everything your foster cat needs: food, litter, a carrier, bowls, toys, and all veterinary care. You provide the love and the space. Some foster parents choose to purchase extras, but it's never required. If your foster cat has a medical issue, we handle it completely.
Yes! Many of our foster families have their own cats and/or dogs. We'll match you with a foster cat that's known to be good with other animals. We do recommend having a separate room where the foster cat can decompress for the first few days before slow introductions begin.
It happens more often than you'd think, and we call it a "foster fail" -- though we think of it as a foster win! Foster families get first priority to adopt their foster cat. You'll still complete the adoption application, but the process is streamlined since we already know the home environment.
No experience is necessary! We provide a foster orientation, a detailed care guide, and ongoing support from experienced volunteers. Whether it's your first time or you're a seasoned foster, you'll have a dedicated foster coordinator you can reach out to anytime with questions or concerns.
Yes, and we especially need bottle-baby fosters during kitten season! Neonatal kittens require feeding every 2-3 hours (including overnight), so this is a bigger time commitment. We provide all supplies and training, and our experienced fosters are available to mentor you through the process. It's one of the most rewarding things you can do.
Fill out our foster application on our website or email us at catsmeowcatrescue@gmail.com. We'll schedule a brief phone interview, go over our foster guidelines, and match you with a cat that fits your living situation and experience level. Most new fosters have a cat in their home within a week or two of applying.

Volunteering

7 questions
We have many ways to help! Beyond fostering, you can volunteer at our PetSmart adoption center in Milwaukie, help with transport runs to veterinary appointments, assist at fundraising events, manage social media posts, photograph cats for adoption listings, do administrative work, or help with our TNR (trap-neuter-return) program for community cats.
We appreciate consistent help, but we understand life is busy. Some roles, like PetSmart adoption center shifts, follow a regular weekly schedule (usually 2-4 hours per week). Other roles, like event help or transport, are on an as-needed basis. We'll work with you to find a role that matches your availability.
Yes! Volunteers ages 14-17 can participate with a signed parental consent form and must be accompanied by an adult for certain activities. Teen volunteers often help at adoption events, assist with cat socialization, and support our social media efforts. It's a great way to earn community service hours.
Yes. All new volunteers attend a brief orientation (usually done via video call) that covers our mission, policies, cat handling basics, and the specific role you're interested in. For specialized roles like TNR or neonatal kitten care, we provide additional hands-on training with experienced team members.
Absolutely! We always need skilled volunteers. Professional photographers help us create compelling adoption listings. Marketing and social media volunteers boost our visibility. Handy volunteers help build cat enclosures for foster homes or set up event spaces. Web developers, grant writers, and graphic designers are also hugely valuable. Reach out and tell us your skills!
Yes! Several of our volunteer roles can be done from home. Data entry, social media management, writing adoption bios, following up on adoption applications, coordinating schedules, and grant research are all remote-friendly tasks. Email us at catsmeowcatrescue@gmail.com to learn more about remote opportunities.
We love corporate volunteer days! Groups can help with event setup, supply drives, community outreach, or special projects. We'll work with your company to plan a meaningful volunteer experience. Contact us at least 2-3 weeks in advance so we can coordinate logistics and make the most of your team's time.

Health & Medical Care

6 questions
Yes, without exception. Every cat adopted through Cat's Meow is spayed or neutered, vaccinated (FVRCP and rabies), microchipped, dewormed, treated for fleas, and tested for FIV/FeLV before going to their new home. Kittens too young for surgery at intake are fixed before their adoption is finalized.
FIV (Feline Immunodeficiency Virus) and FeLV (Feline Leukemia Virus) are infectious diseases that affect cats. We test every cat that enters our rescue. Cats that test positive receive special care and are adopted to homes that understand their needs. FIV-positive cats can live long, healthy lives with proper care. FeLV-positive cats need indoor-only homes and monitoring.
Contact us right away! If it's within the 14-day health guarantee period, we'll coordinate with our vet partners to get your cat seen. For emergencies after hours, take your cat to the nearest emergency vet clinic and let us know as soon as possible. We stand behind the health of our cats and will help however we can.
Yes! We participate in TNR (trap-neuter-return) efforts in the Milwaukie and Gladstone areas. We can loan humane traps, help coordinate spay/neuter appointments through low-cost clinics, and provide guidance on managing community cat colonies. If you're feeding stray cats in your neighborhood, reach out and we'll help you get them fixed.
If you've found a stray cat, contact us and we'll try to help. We'll check for a microchip, post on local lost-and-found pages, and, if space permits, take the cat into our foster program. Please note that as a small rescue we often run at capacity, but we can always provide advice and connect you with additional resources.
While we don't operate a clinic, we partner with several low-cost spay/neuter programs in the Portland metro area including the Oregon Humane Society and Feral Cat Coalition of Oregon. We can refer you to affordable options and, in some cases, help subsidize the cost for community cats or families in financial hardship. Contact us for details.
