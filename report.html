Section 1

Executive Summary

The new Cat's Meow Cat Rescue website is a complete ground-up redesign that replaces the current 7-page Wix site with a 166-page, feature-rich platform purpose-built for a foster-based cat rescue. Every element — from the custom cat paw cursor to the 160-article blog — is designed to accomplish three things: help more cats get adopted, increase donations, and grow our volunteer and foster network.

The prototype is fully functional and live for review. It includes an online shop, interactive adoption application, live Petfinder integration showing all 31 currently adoptable cats, a personality quiz to match visitors with their ideal cat, an events calendar, fundraising progress trackers, and a comprehensive blog covering every topic a cat owner or potential adopter might search for.

Key Achievement We went from 7 pages with no blog, no shop, no FAQ, and no interactive features — to 166 pages with 160 blog articles, 50 FAQs, 11 shop products, a personality quiz, live cat profiles, and 24 interactive features. Google Lighthouse scores improved by an average of +38.5 points across all four categories.

Section 6

Why 160 Blog Posts Matter for SEO

This is the most important strategic decision in the entire website. Here's exactly why 160 blog articles are the single highest-value investment in this redesign:

How Search Engines Find Websites

Google ranks websites based on the number of relevant, quality pages it can index. The current Wix site has ~7 pages. That means Google has 7 chances to show our site in search results. The new site has 166 pages — that's 166 separate doorways from Google to our website.

Real-world example: Someone in Portland searches "how to introduce two cats." Our article "Multi-Cat Household Tips" appears in Google results. They read it, discover we're a local rescue, see 31 adoptable cats, and start an adoption application — all from a Google search that had nothing to do with adoption.

The Long-Tail Keyword Strategy

Generic searches like "cat rescue" are dominated by national organizations with huge budgets. We can't compete there. But long-tail searches — specific, local, question-based queries — have less competition and higher intent. Our 160 articles target these searches specifically:

The Portland/Oregon Local Advantage

Many of our articles specifically target Portland, Oregon, and the Milwaukie/Gladstone area. This is deliberate. National cat care content competes with PetMD, ASPCA, and other massive sites. But content about cat adoption in Milwaukie, Oregon? There's almost no competition for those searches. We can rank #1 for dozens of local searches.

Content as a Trust Signal

Google's E-E-A-T framework (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) rewards sites that demonstrate deep knowledge in their subject area. A 7-page site says "we exist." A 166-page site with 160 in-depth articles says "we are experts in cat care, adoption, and rescue." Google ranks experts higher.

Compounding Returns

Blog articles are evergreen. An article published today continues to drive traffic for years. Unlike social media posts that disappear in 24 hours, or paid ads that stop working when you stop paying, blog content compounds over time. Every month, Google finds more of our articles, ranks them higher, and sends more visitors — all for free.

JSON-LD Structured Data

The website uses two types of Google-recognized structured data schemas:

  • NonprofitOrganization (homepage) — tells Google we're a registered 501(c)(3) with name, EIN, address, phone, social links, founding date, and area served
  • FAQPage (FAQ page) — all 50 Q&A pairs are marked up so Google can display them as rich snippets directly in search results, taking up more real estate on the results page
What are rich snippets? When someone searches "cat adoption fees portland" and our FAQ page appears in results, Google can show the question AND answer directly on the search results page — without the person even clicking. This makes our result dramatically larger and more clickable than competitors' plain blue links.

Section 9

Custom 404 Error Page

When someone visits a broken link or types a wrong URL, they see a branded error page instead of a generic browser error.

  • Heading: "This cat wandered off!"
  • Subtext: "The page you're looking for doesn't exist or has been moved."
  • Three navigation links: Go Home, Adopt a Cat, About Us
  • Dark mode support
  • meta robots: noindex (correct SEO practice — error pages should not appear in search)
Why it matters: A custom 404 page keeps visitors on the site instead of hitting the browser's back button. It turns a dead end into a redirect to useful content.
